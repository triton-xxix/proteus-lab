# P-0074: does an honest OnlyFans management agency clear its costs? (9 Oct 2026)

Called shot: only if the creators already earn well, which is the part nobody can supply.

## How the money flows (sources are mostly agencies selling the service; treat as ranges)
- The platform keeps 20% of gross. Agencies take 25-50% of what is left (some quote on gross, which
  is a quarter more); chat-only services 10-20%.
- Chatters are paid 10-20% of the sales they make in DMs, or 15-30 dollars an hour.
- Creator income is extremely skewed: the headline median is about 150 dollars a month because it
  counts inactive accounts; active creators are put at 1,000-1,500 a month; the top 1% reportedly take
  about a third of payouts. None of these figures comes from OnlyFans itself.

## The honesty problem, which is the business model
The common agency model has staff or AI write DMs as the creator. OnlyFans' terms bar AI-written
chats and deceptive conduct; Reuters (2024) found the tools sold openly to agencies and not disclosed
to fans; class actions followed (one dismissed in May 2026 on contract and Section 230 grounds, another
reportedly heading to trial in 2027). An honest agency does not impersonate, so it loses the DM sales
the others lean on. I did not research UK law tonight (consumer protection on misleading practices,
and the welfare side of managing adult performers); that part is unverified.

## The model (`model.py`: one manager at 3,000 a month per ten creators, tools 150, ads 100 a creator)
- At the active median (1,250 gross a month), a ten-creator agency at a 40% split loses about 150
  a month; at 50% it makes about 850. Break-even is about 1,300 gross per creator.
- At 5,000 gross per creator it works: about 12,000 a month profit from ten creators at 40%.
- The supercar line needs about 15,600 dollars a month. At median creators no number of creators gets
  there, because each one leaves roughly nothing after its share of a manager.

Verdict: not worth it. The honest version is a staffing business whose profit depends entirely on
signing creators who already earn 5,000 a month or more, and those creators are what every agency
competes for, usually with the DM-impersonation uplift an honest agency refuses to sell.
