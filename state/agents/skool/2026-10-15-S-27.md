# S-27: Lead Gen Jay: Claude Code x Cold Email (58 min) and running the machine (campaigns, ghost ink, testing, replies, scaling)
## What it teaches
- The 58-minute video is a chain of Claude Code skills that calls a vendor API at each stage: buy domains and mailboxes, scrape and verify leads into a free cloud database, write a strategy doc, write copy through a self-grading loop, then push the A/B variants into the sequencer and log results back to the database.
- Two "secrets": a self-grading skill (generate, score against his rules, rewrite the skill, repeat; based on Karpathy's autoresearch) and "reverse lead magnets" (offer to build the prospect a custom list or tool instead of asking for a call).
- Ghost ink (separate video, list marketing, not cold email): a roughly 300-word honest-looking paragraph hidden in the email HTML so the filter reads a personal email, one block per email, never reused, hidden with several CSS tricks at once. Placement moves promotions to inbox only; it does not fix a bad domain, IP or list.
- The Vimeo lessons are operating rules: change one variable per test, audiences in separate campaigns and copy variants inside one, pick winners on opportunities not replies, open and link tracking off, reply within 5 to 10 minutes, scale by adding mailboxes (25 a day each, under 50), work out emails per booked call first, and pause a campaign when reply rate drops overnight.
- Benchmarks he gives: 1 to 2% reply is good, over 5% is high only if the replies are positive, under 1% is low. Everything is self-reported from his own agencies.

## One thing worth trying
Ghost-ink scanner, about 20 minutes, stdlib Python only, in /Users/triton/PROTEUS/sandbox/: parse any saved .eml or HTML email, find elements hidden with the listed tricks (display none, font-size 0, max-height 0, opacity 0, transparent colour, mso-hide), count the words inside them, and flag any hidden block over about 60 words (a normal preheader is under 100 characters). Also hash hidden blocks across a folder to show reuse. Run it on synthetic fixtures built in the script, plus any real newsletter .eml Luke chooses to drop in. This tests whether the tactic is as widespread as he says and gives Triton a way to audit a client's own list mail. Small: one file, no accounts, no keys, no spend. It does not test his inbox-placement claim, which needs seed inboxes.

## Claims to check
- "50 out of 50 tests. If you do this trick, they all land": Proteus cannot send, so ask Luke for a one-off: Triton sends 20 matched pairs (with and without the block) to Luke's own Gmail, and Proteus reads the tab counts over read-only IMAP. Note his own cold-email lesson turns open tracking off, yet this video proves ghost ink with open rates of 99% and 45%, which bot and proxy opens inflate.
- "go from like a 2% average reply rate to our clients to a 5%": the same video later says 1% to 5%, and the Vimeo lesson says anything over 5% usually comes from a tiny targeted list or a signal trigger. Check by asking what the denominator was and whether the replies counted were positive; no public data exists to test it.
- "sees an immediate two to three X in the reply rates": the qualification claim. Testable only by splitting one real list into qualified and unqualified halves; for Proteus that means a paper design with a due date, not a run.

## Skill or workflow candidate
Yes, for Triton's clients (firms with outbound sales teams, plus plumbing and HVAC trades) and as a prospect demo, not for Proteus itself. A "cold email strategy brief" skill: interview the client, then produce the segments, pain points, buying triggers, a qualification prompt, a four-step sequence with one-variable A/B variants and a self-grading pass, plus his scale calculator (emails per booked call, 25 a day per mailbox, 4 mailboxes per domain) and the reply-speed and follow-up rules as a checklist. It needs only Claude and the client's answers to draft; deploying needs the client's sequencer, mailboxes and leads. Do not include ghost ink: it is a hiding technique aimed at a filter, and he himself warns about the line between honest and deceptive hidden text.
