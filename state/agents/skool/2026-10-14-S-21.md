# S-21: Matt Ryder F The GuRUs: the sales process (Sales Fundamentals and Scripting text, Start Here trackers)

## What it teaches
- A call is a fixed sequence: connect (why are you here), situation (what are you doing to get the thing), problem, solution (specific goal plus emotional payoff), consequence of inaction, commitment, then presentation. Each stage gathers one piece of information, then loops back for it two or three times.
- Narrow the question so the call stays short: ask "what are you doing to get better leads", not "what are you doing for marketing". Rabbit holes feel productive and change nothing. Pitch inside about 40 minutes.
- The "awning drop": before the consequence stage, ask "on a scale of one to ten, how close are you with what you have today?" and "why not a ten?". It stops pushback when you move from the dream to the cost of doing nothing.
- Neutral language ("maybe", "could be") is for blunt people only; the passive should skip it. Memorise one script word for word, split into phase index cards (first and last question each), and open identically every call.
- Method for improving: be consistent first, then change one thing at a time, judge process not outcomes ("slumps are a myth", group the shots before adjusting). Track a funnel: pickup to conversation (60 seconds plus), conversation to booking, show rate, pickup rate by hour. Fix the top of the funnel first.

## One thing worth trying
Build a small "consistency and funnel check" for a sales team's call data, as a pitch demo for Triton's M1 lane. In /Users/triton/PROTEUS/sandbox/: one Python 3 stdlib script (about 80 lines) that reads a CSV of calls (timestamp, opener text, picked_up, talk_seconds, booked, showed) and prints (a) pickup rate by hour, (b) pickup-to-conversation rate (talk_seconds 60 or more) and conversation-to-booking and show rates, (c) how many distinct openers there are, using a similarity ratio from difflib, since Matt says a team cannot be coached with varying openers. Test it on 200 invented rows. Size: small, about 20 to 30 minutes, no accounts, keys or spend. Honest limit: invented data proves the code runs, not that a firm needs it. A second small output: a one-page set of phase index cards from the Red Book of Questions (first and last question per stage) as a demo asset.

What a Triton pitch to a sales team should sound like, taken from how these teams are taught: lead with their own funnel numbers (pickup to conversation, show rate, best hour), not with AI. Ask permission, neutral wording: "Would it make sense to look at how consistently your reps open and where calls drop off?" One change at a time: offer a single measured pilot, such as end-of-call capture, not a platform.

## Claims to check
- "pick up to conversation over 60 seconds, like 80%": a good-rep target for the opener. Check against published cold-call benchmarks and, if M1 firms share data, against theirs. A mature team at 80 percent looks high.
- "one in five, which is crazy. On most systems, you're like one in 20": dialler pickup rate on warm leads, 17 to 18 percent. Check against public outbound benchmarks (UK cold-call pickup is plausibly 5 percent or less) before quoting it to a prospect.
- "He has like a 60 to 70% close rate" (a coached fitness closer at about 4,200 AOV, Instagram DM leads): a testimonial with no data shown. Check by asking for the sheet; until then treat as marketing, not evidence.
