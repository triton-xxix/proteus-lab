# S-38: Coach Cayden's outbound sessions, picked by content (the four with the most dialling talk)
## What it teaches
- Four live Q&A and role-play sessions (March 23, May 26, June 29, July 17), 37 to 59 minutes each. Almost all of it is what to say once someone picks up, not dialling mechanics.
- Gatekeeper method (June 29, March 23): ask for the person by first name as if you know them, say "I'll just leave a voicemail" if he is busy, and if asked "is he expecting you" say you do not know. For a cold switchboard, name-drop a vague earlier contact ("they told me to reach out to you") and ask who handles the topic. Casual, confident, "joyful" tone is the point.
- Opener and call control (March 23): "this is a really quick call to see if it even makes sense", then stop to confirm their email so they learn you lead and they answer. Direct opening beats soft for business owners; he treats outbound as sorting, so expect more no's than yeses.
- Follow-up and funnel facts: Loom or email first, call again the moment the Loom is viewed (June 29); more touch points after the sale to cut failed payments (May 26); work backwards from income to appointments to dials (July 17).
- The rest is "identity" framing (tie the prospect's stated goal to who they are, then ask why they have not acted) and framing questions. It is persuasion technique, not a tool.

## One thing worth trying
In /Users/triton/PROTEUS/sandbox/, a stdlib Python script that scans these four transcripts, splits them into speaker-less segments by timestamp, and counts how much of each session is actually about dialling, gatekeepers, voicemail and pick-up numbers versus sales-conversation or identity talk. About 40 lines, 10 minutes, no accounts. Small; the value is a cleaner benchmark table than reading by hand, and a reusable way to pick Skool sessions.

## Claims to check
- "70% of those go to trial" (June 29's law-enforcement caller, captain stage, US police departments): self-reported on one caller's list. Check by asking for a client's own stage-to-stage numbers, not by trusting it.
- "Somewhere between 5 to 10" pick-up rate for insurance (May 26): and close "15 to 20" on fresh leads, "3 to 5" on aged leads. Check against a UK client's dialler export or published UK B2B benchmarks.
- "out of 26 pickups, I got four books on 170" (July 17): about 15 percent pick-up and 15 percent pick-up-to-book on a new setter's first days, one data point. Check by running the same ratios on a real UK call log, since consumer-lead lists and cold B2B differ.

## Skill or workflow candidate
Triton's clients (firms with outbound sales teams, also trades with lead-chasing callers): a "call review from recordings" workflow. It would transcribe a client's recorded calls locally with faster-whisper, tag where each call lost the prospect (opener, qualification, objection, close), and return a one-page report per caller plus the dials-to-booking arithmetic. It needs the client's recordings and consent to process them, no keys. Also a gatekeeper-and-opener script pack for UK trades, but only written from the client's own calls, not copied from these sessions. Do not build the identity-mirror and "stack open loops" persuasion tactics into anything client-facing; Cayden himself calls parts of it "dark arts". The 15 Oct S-31 digest reached the same line.

UK differences, from general knowledge rather than these files, so verify before use: the sessions are US consumer and small-business calling. In the UK, cold calls to companies go through CTPS and to sole traders and individuals through TPS (PECR plus UK GDPR), there is no TCPA-style hours table, and a mobile or local-number rotation trick is likelier to attract Ofcom attention (caller ID must be a valid, callable number). Swearing as a rapport trick ("shit") will land differently on UK trades and gatekeepers. Name-dropping a contact you cannot name is a pretext and could breach honesty rules in a regulated sale; treat it as a technique to avoid.
