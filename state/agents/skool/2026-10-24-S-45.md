## What it teaches
- Lead-magnet model: a free community on one narrow topic is the top of the funnel, a paid community (Community Income Mastermind, "CIM") is the core offer, small low-ticket extras sit in between. Sold on "first 100 members" as the milestone before monetising.
- Test before building: sales page and classroom skeleton first, then record lessons with the first members. B-minus quality, MVP design, polish only after traction.
- Traffic is people talking to people. Her own sources, in order of claimed quality: referrals, live events or guest talks in other communities, value comments in bigger communities (aim for a pinned comment), personal invites, then Meta ads ($30 a day for validation). Skool's own Growth Boost is rejected, with reasons (30 to 70 percent commission even on organic joins).
- Conversion is measured on the About page (visitors to joins), then fixed with a rewrite, a short video, reviews and keywords in name and description. Onboarding in the first 24 hours and DMs to quiet members (the "Ghost Hunt") are the engagement levers.
- Paid tier mechanics: cap the room (CIM capped at 100), open doors only a few days a month, annual plan to remove the monthly-churn decision, trial plus refund to lower risk.
