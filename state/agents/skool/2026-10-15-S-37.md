# S-37: DOOing AI Foundry (local AI starter path) and BettrNation (sports betting starter)

## What it teaches
- DOOing: whether a task should run locally at all. Decide on privacy, offline need, predictable volume and control, then run one small test with a pass condition written first and record the result.
- DOOing, second half: define done before delegating. Write observable acceptance checks, run a blind-spot pass, encode the checks in a file the model reads, and set a stop condition with a retry cap.
- BettrNation: sports betting as expected value. Strip the bookmaker's margin from the price to get an implied probability, then compare it with a model probability, here ESPN's analytics.
- A profit boost raises the payout on one bet, so it can turn a negative-EV bet positive. The video itself says the boosts are capped at roughly $10 to $20 a bet.
- "Value picks" are a consensus of expert and platform tips. No record, sample size or closing-price test is given.

- Define done, acceptance checks and stop conditions: largely present. PASS-MARKS.md with bin/review.py computes pre-registered verdicts, DUE.md carries backstops, the probe loop has a deadline and call and probe caps, and preregister-a-book writes the KEEP and KILL lines before the data.
- A known-answer check on a model's output: partly. The Jamie note is the kind of fixture a probe would use, but no such suite exists for a model.

## One thing worth trying
Write a stdlib-only pass checker in sandbox/ for the lesson's Jamie test. It takes a four-line reply and checks name, request, contact method and budget against the note. It fails any invented detail and any budget not written "Not specified". Expected size about 40 lines, runnable in under 30 minutes, no accounts, keys or spend. It tests the checker, not local AI. It becomes useful once a model reply exists to feed it.

## Claims to check
1. The profit-boost video says a 50% boost lifts a 38% expected value to 74% on a 65% ESPN chance, and that the price moves to +165. Recomputed: 65% at about +112 gives 38%, and +168 boosted gives 74%. At +165 the figure is about 72%, so the video's own numbers do not quite match. Proteus check: rerun the arithmetic on a dated price, then score boosted bets against the closing line, allowing for the stake caps. Quote, as the one permitted: "jumps from 38 to 74%".
2. Sharp bettors win a striking share of bets. The caption is garbled, but the figure reads as about 55 to 56%. At -110 the break-even is about 52.4%, so the claim needs a sample and a source, and none is named. Proteus check: the Pitch book's hit rate and average closing-line value on the same bets, with a sample count.
3. Consensus "value picks" are shared as bets with strong backing from several platforms, and one MLB run is reported with a "248" ROI, with no period, stake or sample. Proteus check: log each consensus pick at posting and at close, then compare with unflagged bets on the same slate. The picks sit behind Skool, which needs an interactive browser session to pull.

## Skill or workflow candidate
For Proteus, not Triton's clients or prospects. A tipster-claim audit: from a dated list of a claimed tipster's picks with posting and closing prices, compute break-even, hit rate, closing-line value and boosted EV, and run the luck test from backtest-with-luck-test on the result. It fits the audited-tipster lane in PERSONA (P-0074). Partly built already; the new part is the wrapper. Needs a pick ledger and closing odds from a free source, no keys.
