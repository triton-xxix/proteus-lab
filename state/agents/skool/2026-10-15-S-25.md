# S-25: Lead Gen Jay: list building, verification, qualification, offers (Google Maps scraping, 10% reply claim)

## What it teaches
- Reply rate follows how rarely a prospect is cold emailed: Apollo and LinkedIn-sourced decision makers are saturated, so go to people who sit outside those databases (local businesses from Google Maps, creators, Instagram bios) and expect more replies.
- Build the list with tight filters (job title, company keywords rather than industry, headcount as a proxy for revenue, verified email), then verify, then have an AI say yes or no on fit per lead, which he says cuts 30 to 50 percent of the list.
- Verification is a two-step: a cheap bulk verifier first, then a catch-all checker for the "risky" bucket, because he says about half of risky addresses are deliverable. Bounces and spam complaints damage the sending domain.
- Google Maps scraping comes in three tiers: a paid app, an Apify actor (about $2 per 1,000 places), or an open-source scraper on a server with residential proxies. Maps gives phone, website and rating but often only role-based emails (info@, hello@), and no owner name.
- Offers: for a boring, crowded service, lead with a front-end offer (a loss leader at cost, or a non-sales pretext such as a journalist or partnership) to get a reply, then upsell the real retainer. The goal of email one is a raised hand, not a sale.

## What we would need to fetch
- Free first: OpenStreetMap Overpass API (keyless) for UK plumbers and heating firms, plus each firm's own website for a published contact address. Companies House data for age of company (his "younger firms reply more" rule), which is also free.
- Free tier, needs a key: Google Places API (New), already on the shelf.
- Open-source Google Maps scraper from GitHub: not needed to test the idea. It needs proxies and a server, and Google blocks scraping, so it is a poor fit under the charter.
- Paid, only if a test earns it: Million Verifier (credits), FindEmail, Trusted Leads. Apify at $2 per 1,000 places is cheap but needs an account.
- Mailbox checking (MX lookup) can be done with Python's standard library and a DNS query, no service.

## One thing worth trying
Overpass versus Jay's Google Maps claim, for Triton's actual targets. In /Users/triton/PROTEUS/sandbox/ write one small Python script (about 40 lines, standard library only) that queries the Overpass API for craft=plumber and the heating/HVAC tags in three UK cities, then counts the share of results that have a name, website, phone and email tag. Add an MX-record check on the domain of any website found, and report how many reachable firms carry a usable address. Under 30 minutes, no account, no key, no spend, one JSON file out. Result: how many contactable plumbing and HVAC firms a free keyless source gives per city, against his "15 to 20 percent have a contact email" for Maps. It is a count of coverage, not a contact list, and nothing is emailed. UK note: sole traders are individuals under PECR, so a list of these is a research artefact, not an outreach list.
