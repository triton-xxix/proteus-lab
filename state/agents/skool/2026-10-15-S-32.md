# S-32: Matt Ryder: running a sales team (Coach Jack sampled 2 of 30, Coach Mike 2 of 14) and the sales-management PDFs
## What it teaches
- Sales managers fix the funnel before the people: a "MIND" (most important numbers and drivers) chain runs dials, pick-up, conversation, booked call, show-up, close, cash. Fix the leakiest early stage first, one change at a time, with enough volume to see a pattern.
- Calls are reviewed on a 1 to 5 scorecard across five pillars (tonality, command of the call, probing, scripting, objection handling), with timestamps and one or two action points, then drilled by 5 to 10 minute role-plays with one piece of feedback each.
- Between booking and the call, the prospect gets a thank-you page with proof (before and after, case studies) and a short triage call. The claim is that this lifts show-up and close rates more than script changes do.
- Follow-up after a stalled call uses a concrete next step ("conditions of satisfaction") and a booked second call, not "just checking in". Objections are sorted into money, confidence or fear, in that order.
- The sample is coaching Q&A and call reviews for high-ticket info and coaching offers (trading, bizopp, dialer software), not trades or local services. The PDFs are generic templates with blank numbers, not data.

## Tools they use
- GoHighLevel (GHL) as CRM and dialer, and Kixie, both named in passing and run down as weak ("tier 3 network"). No URLs given.
- A purpose-built power dialer sold by the community (Dialer, speed-to-lead routing). No URL given.

- Typeform and calendar booking pages as the opt-in and booking steps; Trustpilot and Google reviews linked from the thank-you page.
- The PDFs name nothing beyond "CRM" and an end-of-day (EOD) report form.

## One thing worth trying
Build `/Users/triton/PROTEUS/sandbox/funnel_mind.py`, a stdlib-only calculator that reproduces the Low Hanging Fruit funnel (dials, pick-up, conversation, booked, show-up, close, cash per sale) and ranks which stage to fix first, showing both a relative lift and a percentage-point lift on each stage so the comparison is fair. Then rerun it with a trades-style inbound funnel (enquiry, answered, quote booked, quote visit, won) using plain placeholder numbers. Size: about 60 lines, 20 minutes, no keys or spend. It also checks the PDF's own arithmetic (see claims).

## Claims to check
- "perceives intent and meaning 96% of it from everything other than the words" (Jack, Sept 15). Looks like the widely misquoted Mehrabian 7/38/55 finding, which came from tiny studies of single words and feelings, not whole sales calls. Check by searching the primary source, then say so in Field Notes.
- "Total Commission earned from EXACT SAME stats except a 20% pick up" gives $42,000 against $20,000. The sums hold (84 sales at $500), but a 10 to 20% pick-up is a doubling while the close-rate comparison is 33 to 43%, a 30% rise, so "easier than close rate" is an apples-to-oranges framing. Also the PDF says 120 calls taken, but 198 booked at 65% show is about 129. Check with the sandbox calculator.
- "this will push your shop rate past like 70% for the sales call" (Jack, triage plus proof). One coach's assertion, no data shown. Proteus can only check it on a client's own booked-versus-shown numbers before and after; no way to test it alone.

## Skill or workflow candidate
Two, for Triton's clients (firms with outbound sales teams, M1 lane), both also usable as a prospect demo:
- Call review scorer: takes a transcript, scores the five pillars 1 to 5 with timestamps and writes two action points, in the format of the PDF. Needs call transcripts (whisper locally) and the client's consent. No keys beyond Claude.
- Funnel leak finder: takes a client's weekly counts (dials, answered, booked, shown, closed) and returns the stage with the biggest gain per point of improvement plus the monthly report skeleton. Needs a CSV or CRM export only.
Neither is for Proteus itself.
