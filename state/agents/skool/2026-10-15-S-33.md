# S-33: Matt Ryder: AI in sales and agency selling (Coach Jordan Park sampled 3 of 13, Coach Aaron 2 of 20, one call review)

## What it teaches
Mostly sales craft, with a thin AI layer. The Jordan Park sessions are the useful ones: front-load outbound dials (4 attempts 30 minutes apart on day one, then hourly) instead of one dial a day for ten days, and kill follow-up buckets whose conversion rate is zero.
Use AI as a slow-lane worker: a text-message setter for the long tail of dead or "not now" leads, and reusable Claude skills that grade call transcripts the same way every time.
The pre-call and no-book text sequences are low-friction questions, never links ("want me to send the link?"), from a named person, over 12 to 14 days.
Aaron and the call review teach cold-call framing: assume the prospect is unaware, never say "what we do", interject early, and ask questions without commenting. This is human technique, not AI.
Sample caveat: the Jordan Park 23 June file is 86 minutes but the first 8 minutes are blank audio; Aaron 001 is a two-minute promo; Aaron 020 and the call review are mostly mindset and live coaching. Three of six files are loose group calls, so the sample is weak on tooling and fairly representative of the library's tone.

## One thing worth trying
Cadence simulator, about 20 minutes, no accounts or spend. In /Users/triton/PROTEUS/sandbox/ write one Python script that takes a lead list size, a daily dial capacity and a assumed per-attempt pick-up probability that decays by attempt number and day, then compares "100 leads once a day for 5 days" with "30 leads, 4 dials a day over 3 days" on expected connects. Inputs are assumptions, so the output is a sizing aid for a Triton pitch, not evidence. Size: one 40-line script, one table. Its real value is giving Triton a number to put in front of a sales manager. It proves nothing about real pick-up rates, which only the client's dialer data can show.

## Skill or workflow candidate
Yes, one, for Triton's clients (firms running outbound sales teams) and as a prospect demo: a "call grader" skill. It takes a call transcript, scores it against a fixed rubric (opening frame, did the rep sound like a salesperson, talk-to-listen ratio, red flags the rep skipped, objection first appearing in the first ten minutes) and returns the same grade each time. The Jordan Park tip that skills beat re-typed prompts because outputs stay consistent is the mechanism. Needs: call transcripts from the client (compliance and consent matter, one reviewer says their employer bans downloading recordings), a rubric the client signs off, and nothing paid. Secondary candidate, lower value: a "dial cadence audit" that reads a dialer export and reports connect rate by day number, so dead-day dials can be cut. That one needs the client's export.
