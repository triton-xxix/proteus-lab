## What it teaches
- Launch ladder: start the community on Skool's freemium model (standard tier free), reach 30 to 50 members for social proof, then switch to tiers at $9 a month or $49 a year (about 55% off annual, so most buyers take annual and cash comes up front). Later tiers: $27 or $149 a year, VIP as a done-with-you day or a few private calls, first five at a launch price.
- Attraction offer: a gamified 10-minute-a-day challenge with templates, monthly prizes and a leaderboard. Engagement comes from pinning an open-ended post to each lesson page ("pin to course page"), short daily updates and a prize.
- Ascension is built into Skool: classroom courses and calendar workshops are locked by tier, so members upgrade by clicking. Freemium members never see the paid tiers on joining (until a July 2026 Skool update), so one of three pinned posts must be a standing VIP or premium offer.
- Traffic: organic posts naming the community, Skool Discovery keywords (11 allowed), commenting helpfully in other communities (the "school network", about 20% of his sign-ups, high conversion), workshops, and Facebook ads ($2 to $5 a day) using cartoon "how did you get so many clients?" creatives. Growth Boost (Skool-run ads for a commission) gave him $202 new MRR in 30 days.
- Mechanism behind the offer funnel: a 30-minute paid or free workshop, replay resold as a $7 product with bump and upsells, then a high-ticket offer. Judge it as a funnel, not a pitch: front end liquidates ad spend, back end carries the profit.

## Claims to check
- "one low ticket sale per day at $49 ... $17,640 per year" (1 Dec 2025). 49 x 360 = 17,640, so it is 360 days, and it assumes one new annual buyer every day with no churn; check by modelling with a realistic sign-up rate.
- "an additional twenty eight thousand six hundred and sixty eight dollars" (4 Jan 2026). That is 12 x the first 30 days' net cash (about $2,400), which includes annual payments that only recur once a year.
