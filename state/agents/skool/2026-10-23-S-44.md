## What it teaches
- Start freemium (free standard tier, paid Premium and VIP), grow to 40 to 50 members so the page does not look like a ghost town, then switch to tiers with a cheap standard tier ($9/month or $49/year, annual about 55% off). Each free member is a lead to work towards paid or high ticket.
- Skool is the nurture layer and a 30-minute live workshop is the conversion step: community for touch points, workshop for the upgrade to a mid or high ticket offer.
- Traffic is one templated post a day (about 10 minutes, AI-assisted), repurposed short videos with a "comment WORD for the link" call to action, a weekly promo thread, commenting in other Skool groups, and $2 to $5 a day Meta ads pointed straight at the about page (it is the funnel).
- Set up for discovery: 11 keywords under Settings, Discovery; the community name repeated in the about text, one Instagram caption and one YouTube video so Google and AI overviews link it to the founder.
- Retention and conversion levers: 7-day free trial with three pinned posts, lock some lessons for trial users, a biweekly anti-churn post, auto-DM onboarding, and a courtesy DM when a card expires.

## Claims to check
- "1 sale per day at $49 = $17,640 per year": that is 360 days; he later writes $17,885 (365 days). Check: arithmetic only, 49 x 365 = 17,885. Shows the headline is rounded and assumes one new annual buyer every single day.
- "98% retention rate" past the free trial, and "66% free-trial conversion rate": quoted as his own, from a community priced at $9. Check: a client community's Skool dashboard retention and trial conversion over at least 60 days; $9 makes cancelling unlikely so this will not transfer to a $47 or $97 price.
- "Skool gave me an additional $202/month recurring" via Growth Boost, "95% retention": one month, one community. Check: Skool's Growth Boost terms and a live community's attribution split (S-43 already notes Growth Boost traffic was once wrongly counted as direct).
Also noted: grand-opening "$1,065 collected" with "$941 annual" plus "$159 monthly" adds to $1,100, not $1,065, so the figures mix cash and recurring.
