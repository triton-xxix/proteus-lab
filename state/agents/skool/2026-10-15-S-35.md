# S-35: Rebel Economist (Steve Keen, free): the sampler course and the free books

## What it teaches
- Money is bank liabilities moving through double-entry bookkeeping. A bank loan raises loans and deposits together, so lending creates money. The money multiplier and loanable funds are called wrong.
- Macro models can be built from true-by-definition ratios (employment rate, wage share, private debt ratio) turned into growth-rate equations by log differentiation, then simulated. No microfoundations.
- Goodwin's wage/employment cycle plus a private debt ratio gives a "good" equilibrium and a "bad" one. Rising cycles can fall into the bad basin, where employment goes to zero and debt/GDP goes to infinity. Counter-cyclical government deficit spending removes that outcome. Adding falling prices gives Fisher's debt deflation.
- The empirical hook: credit (the change in private debt, as a share of GDP) moves opposite to unemployment. The 2008 crisis is the case, and the book also claims panics of 1837 and the 1930s followed attempts to run down government debt.
- The rest is polemic against neoclassical economics, plus an upsell to the paid 7-week challenge.

## Tools they use
- Minsky: free open-source system dynamics program with "Godley tables" for money flows. https://sourceforge.net/projects/minsky/
- Ravel: Keen's successor and data tool, paid on Patreon ($1 a month for simulation, $7 for data analysis per the book). https://www.patreon.com/ravelation
- BIS bulk credit data, used for the private debt series. https://data.bis.org/static/bulk/WS_TC_csv_col.zip
- US Treasury historical debt data. https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/historical-debt-outstanding/historical-debt-outstanding
- Rethinking Economics, student group. https://www.rethinkeconomics.org/
- Substack and Patreon pages for Keen (profstevekeen), named but not needed.

## One thing worth trying
Rebuild the 3-state Goodwin-Keen model (employment rate, wage share, private debt ratio; equation 34 in the Elon book) in sandbox/ with scipy solve_ivp, in a single file of about 60 lines. Run it twice: private sector only, and with the government deficit term added (equation 43). The called shot, written before running: with some starting points and linear Phillips and investment functions the private-only run falls into the bad equilibrium (debt ratio rising without bound, employment toward zero), and the same start with deficit spending does not. Size: small, 30 minutes or less, no accounts, no keys, no spend. The thing it tests is whether the "crash" is a property of the equations or of hand-picked parameters. So also scan a grid of starting debt ratios and count how many collapse. That count is the artefact.

## Claims to check
- "went from 15% of GDP in 2006 down to minus 5% in 2009" (US credit, transcript 005). Check: BIS total credit to the private non-financial sector, year-on-year change in the stock divided by GDP, from the bulk file above.
- "staggering minus 0.93 between 1990 and 2015" (credit versus unemployment, Elon book). Check: the same BIS series against FRED unemployment, same window, and whether the figure survives detrending, since two smooth series can correlate strongly by trend alone.
- "no less than 89% of firms" have constant or falling marginal costs (Sampler, Session 3). Hard to check cheaply. Blinder's survey is cited as the source (11% rising, about half constant, 40% falling), so the 89% is Blinder's number restated. Check by reading Blinder 1998 pp. 101-102 or a replication, not by data pull.
