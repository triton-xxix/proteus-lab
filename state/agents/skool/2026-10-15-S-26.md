# S-26: Lead Gen Jay: reverse lead magnets and cold email copy

## What it teaches
- A reverse lead magnet changes the cold email ask from "want my PDF?" to "can I spend time on your business and send you something specific?". Perceived value rises because the prospect thinks someone did work for them. The Loom-video trick, automated.
- The delivery is a small web tool ("SaaS magnet") that takes one input already in the lead list (LinkedIn URL, website), scrapes it, has an LLM produce a specific result, and shows it on a page with a booking button. The link is pre-filled, so the result loads on click with no form.
- Fulfilment is automated: a reply classifier tags "interested", drafts a reply with the pre-filled link, and a click is itself a buying signal that can be retargeted with pixels.
- Copy rules: the "triple tap" (open from subject plus first sentence, read from 2 to 3 short sentences naming problem, mechanism and credibility, reply with a one-word yes), three emails maximum, plain text and no links in email one, spintax mandatory, no open tracking, unsubscribe language only from email two.
- Evidence is his own: reply rates of 2 to 8 percent on client campaigns, shown as dashboard screenshots. No method for comparing against a control.

## Skill or workflow candidate
Triton's clients (firms with outbound sales teams) and Luke ourselves: a cold email copy checker skill. It would take a draft sequence and score it against the triple tap rules (preview text does not signal a sale, 2 to 3 short sentences, one-word-yes CTA, six sentences maximum, no links in email one), expand spintax, flag spam words and loop until it passes. Needs nothing but Claude Code and a local word list. A second candidate, the website audit magnet above, would work as a prospect demo for plumbing and HVAC firms and needs a web fetch only. UK caveat for both: his "ignore CAN-SPAM, never add unsubscribe" advice is a US stance. Under PECR, B2B email to limited companies is allowed with a clear sender identity and an opt-out, but sole traders and some partnerships count as individuals who need prior consent, and many small trades firms are exactly that. Any UK version must keep an opt-out and filter the list by company type.
