# S-41: Skool Homeroom classroom: Orientation, First Community Win, Setup Your Skool (4 videos)

## What it teaches
- Setup course for a beginner: list what you know, check people already ask about it (Skool, Facebook groups, Reddit, YouTube, Google), narrow to a specific audience, and write a one-sentence community description.
- Plan on paper first (plan tier, name, public or private, price direction, 3 to 5 action-based categories, named leaderboard levels), then build in about 20 minutes.
- Launch steps: icon, cover, description, about page (character-limited, she trimmed 1058 to 992), categories, level names, a placeholder "new student orientation" course, then invite 3 people and post a welcome with intro prompts and a next step.
- First Community Win is a 7-day diagnostic: destination, post-join path, what members do, the win they get (not just actions), next step, where they stop, then fix one thing. It is a retention funnel audit.
- Orientation is mostly owner proof (Homeroom launched 4 Mar 2026, Discovery rank 5,024 to 293 and 100 members by day 45) and weekly engagement mechanics (Saturday Detention thread, Honor Roll pinned top 5 for a free week of promo).

## Tools they use
- Skool itself (skool.com), Hobby $9/month or Pro $99/month, 14 day free trial on every new community.
- Skoolkit (scheduled posts), Wingman (Skool CRM), Skooly, Skool Extensions, Panda: named on Skool, no URLs beyond skool.com/<slug>/about links.
- Promo venues: The Directory on Skool, The Skool Hub, The Skool Classifieds, The Skool Directory, Pinterest Skool weekend, BYOB, Skool Partners.
- Google Docs/Drive worksheets (Community Idea Validator, Planning Blueprint, Set Up Worksheet, First 7 Days Workbook).

## Claims to check
- "30 members paying 37 bucks a month. And that's $1,110 per month." Arithmetic is right (30 x 37 = 1,110); it ignores the 10% Hobby fee (about $111) and Skool's own cut, and assumes 30 paying members, which is the hard part. Check against real member-count data from other owners.
- "ranked at 293 in discovery and had 100 members" at day 45. Check by looking at the Skool discovery ranking history if a public snapshot exists; sample is one community, early.
- Hobby "10% transaction fee", Pro "2.9%". Check on Skool's current pricing page (fees and the tier names may have changed since recording).

## Skill or workflow candidate

Only additions: the 7-day post-join audit (win, next step, where they stop) as a retention check, and the welcome post template. Needs no keys; data is the client's member list. Low priority, fold into the existing skill rather than build a new one.

## Worth paying for the locked parts?
Mentioned: 37 lessons across seven courses are locked behind paid tiers (Standard $9, Premium $33, VIP $49 a month, per the First Win page) plus a level-gated GEMS Lab. The free part is generic beginner material with little numeric depth, so it does not prove the paid parts are worth money. The locked titles that match our goal (Skool Visibility Guide, Promotion Playbook, Community Growth Essentials) might be, but this free set cannot tell us. The owner posts (451 in the vault) are the better free source.

## Verdict
PARK: sound but basic setup material the frameworks skill already covers; take only the 7-day post-join audit and welcome template, and read the owner posts instead for growth and discovery detail.
