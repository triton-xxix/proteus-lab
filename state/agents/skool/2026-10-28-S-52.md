# S-52: Memecoin rooms: Viper (classroom, 12 videos) and Sniper Skool (watchlists, Grok prompts)
## What it teaches
- Viper teaches launching coins as the "developer": buy first from 5 to 20 bundled wallets so the launcher can always exit at break even, then get traders to buy above them.
- Four ways the dev earns: soft rug (sell in small batches, under 10% of supply a time, 100 to 500 sells), hard rug (sell everything within 2 minutes), pump and dump (paid callers, then sell everything), and pump.fun creator rewards (a cut of volume with no position).
- The "Pump & Dumps" module is an explicit playbook: launch a spin-off of a meta that has already peaked, pay 10 to 20 callers a minute apart, hype fake future promotion, then sell every wallet at once. It is market manipulation of the buyers; the files do not mention a lawful framing.
- Sniper Skool is thin: four classroom items are only sales blurbs for PulseChain "top 5" watchlists, a "secret list", Grok 4 prompts and a cheat sheet. The files carry no list contents. The owner's feed is mostly generic risk posts (approvals, leverage maths, two-bucket sizing).
- Useful observation in the free Viper material: traders move as a herd between metas (dogs, cats, snakes), a meta lasts 1 hour to a week, and a meta is "peaked" when the lead coin falls while many variants exist.

## One thing worth trying
Paper-score the one defensible mechanical claim: "a meme meta has peaked when the lead coin is down more than 50% from its high while 4 or more variants exist; coins launched or bought then underperform those bought on the rise." In /Users/triton/PROTEUS/sandbox/ take the existing Grinder snapshots, tag each name-cluster by lead-coin drawdown and variant count, and compare 1 hour and 6 hour forward returns of variants in "peaked" versus "rising" clusters. No accounts, keys or spend, about 30 minutes, small. Buy-side paper test only. The expected result is that late variants lose, which would be the cleanest evidence that the pump-and-dump module's exit liquidity is the retail buyer.

## Claims to check
- "Making $10,000 a month is ridiculously easy" (owner post, 16 Aug). Check: no verifiable record offered; the same post says the example user "ended up losing money". Treat as marketing; test against the Grinder's own win rate for new launches.
- "Good timing... 10 times higher chance of failing" (launch hour, anatomy video). Check: bucket Grinder snapshots by launch hour UTC against 6 hour return and volume; reject if no hour effect.
- "99% of those coins within the hour pump... then crash" (about 40,000 to 50,000 coins a day). Check: count pump.fun launches per day and share holding above launch price after 1 hour from keyless data.

## Skill or workflow candidate
None. The only repeatable method is the launch playbook, which is a pump and dump or soft rug on other traders, and Proteus will not build it. The meta-peak detector in the test above could become a Proteus Grinder feature, not a client skill.

## Worth paying for the locked parts?
Locked: about 50 Viper items (Staying Safe, Bundling, Marketing, Hard and Soft Rugs, Front Running, Copy Trading Whales, Mentorship) plus Sniper Skool's Founding Member Package. The free part is mostly motivation and one worked pump-and-dump; the paid parts are launch tooling for harming buyers, and the owner says the course is being rebuilt and the price raised. Not worth money for Triton or Proteus. Copy Trading Whales and Front Running are the only trading-side modules, but the wallet list is unverified (see the Grinder insiders work instead).

## Verdict
SKIP: Viper is a pump-and-dump and rug-pull playbook (flagged, not for Proteus to run or fund), and Sniper Skool is sales blurbs with no contents; the only salvage is the meta-peak test, which the Grinder can run from its own data.
