## What it teaches
- Free "30-day hackathon" community as the lead magnet: one classroom, a numbered path, one narrow promise, then a paid community ("Community Income Mastermind") behind it. She says 300 free members in 33 days, 886 at about seven and a half months, 87 paid at $97 a month.
- Early traffic: roughly 45 to 54 percent of signups from Meta ads at $15 then $30 a day (her stats screen), the rest from value-commenting in other Skool communities and from guest workshops and joint events. Her rule: ads are cold water, pair them with a warm source, and push hard to 100 members before monetising.
- About page: be specific to the point of being unglamorous, start the description with a verb and end with an instruction to click, simple logo, name the outcome and the audience, put real search words in the keywords. One member changed "three words" in the who-I-help line and says signups flooded in.
- Pricing ladder she actually ran: $47 got no buyers, dropped to $7, then a monthly step ladder ($17, $27, $37 ... $97 in January), later open-door/closed-door intake capped at 100 members and an annual $497. Her stated reason: nobody knows her, so the first price must be a no-brainer.
- Engagement: hand-pick 10 founding members, weekly Monday goal call, "ghost hunt" (10 personal DMs a day-block to quiet members, sorted by leaderboard level), review swaps via ProofWorth, joint events (12-speaker summit added about 100 members, $27 all-access, about 30 buyers).
