# S-24: Lead Gen Jay: cold email infrastructure and deliverability (domains, DNS, sending tool, warmup, tests, blacklists)

## What it teaches
- Inbox placement is decided by reputation at three levels, in order of weight: sending IP, then domain, then mailbox. Renting Google or Microsoft mailboxes hands the IP problem to them; cheap SMTP resellers leave you owning one shared IP.
- Hygiene you control: SPF, DKIM and DMARC on every sending domain (checkable by lookup), domain age of 30 days minimum, mailboxes warmed from day one, about 100 sends a day per domain (5 mailboxes at 20 to 25 each), tracking and links off.
- Diagnose in a fixed order: DNS scan, then blacklist check, then a real inbox placement test using the real copy. If DNS and blacklists are clean, the copy is the cause. The fix for a burned domain is to pull it from campaigns and warm it for 2 to 4 weeks, or replace it.
- 2026 turn (YouTube video 11): reply rates fell 50 to 60 percent because bulk domains sharing the same Cloudflare nameservers, and all redirecting to one website, were fingerprinted and blacklisted by a domain-based list. His new standard is per-customer isolated nameservers (AWS Route 53) and a masking proxy instead of a redirect. This contradicts his own earlier lesson 06/07 advice, which says nameserver choice and masking make no difference.
- Much of it is a sales funnel into his Instantly links, his paid setup service ($3 a mailbox a month) and his Insiders programme.

## What we would need to fetch
Nothing for the read-only checks: `dig` (already on macOS) covers MX, SPF, DKIM, DMARC and NS lookups, and DNSBL or SURBL lookups are DNS queries too. To actually send you would need domains, Google or Microsoft mailboxes and Instantly (paid, accounts). Not needed for Proteus.

## One thing worth trying
A keyless "outbound readiness audit" script in /Users/triton/PROTEUS/sandbox/: given a list of domains, run `dig` for MX, SPF (TXT), DMARC (_dmarc TXT), DKIM at common selectors (google, selector1, selector2, default), nameserver names, and a DNSBL/SURBL lookup, then print a pass/fail table against his checklist (SPF present, DMARC present, DKIM present, nameservers not Cloudflare, MX points to Google or Microsoft). Run it on the Neptune domains plus 10 to 20 trade-firm domains (plumbing, HVAC, firms with sales teams) to see how many have the basics. Under 30 minutes, no accounts, no keys, no spend. Size: about 80 lines of Python on `subprocess` and `dig`. Caveat: DKIM selectors cannot be enumerated, so a miss is "not found at common selectors", not "absent". Public resolvers may be rate limited on Spamhaus and SURBL lookups.

## Claims to check
1. "Google is still beating Microsoft for cold email" (lesson 05, 2025 recording). Check: not checkable keylessly; would need seed-list tests. Note only that Microsoft vs Google MX share among UK trade firms is measurable with the audit script above.
2. "reply rate drop by about 50, 60%" blamed on shared Cloudflare nameservers (video 11). Check: pull NS records for domains known to be cold-email sending domains versus normal small-business domains and compare how many share an identical Cloudflare nameserver pair; and look up a few on the SURBL list. A shared pair on its own proves little, so treat it as a fingerprint count, not a cause.
3. "200 emails to book one call" versus "1 to 3% reply rate" (calculator defaults vs checklist, in the toolkit). Check: arithmetic only, 0.5% booking against 1 to 3% reply implies 17% to 50% of replies book, which is plausible but unsourced; no independent data in the files.
