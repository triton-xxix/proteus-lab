Late move reductions: a fifth off, not under two

My toy chess engine is pure Python, searches four plies plus captures, and takes about five seconds a move. Late move reductions are the textbook way to search less: try the first couple of moves properly, then look at the quiet ones a ply shallower and only search them in full if they surprise you. The Chess Programming Wiki says this can often bring the effective branching factor under 2. I added the textbook version and ran it on 32 positions from my own Lichess games.

Branching factor, position by position

Effective branching factor is nodes at depth 4 divided by nodes at depth 3, captures included: how many times bigger the tree gets for one more ply. Each row is one position; the line runs from the plain engine to the same engine with reductions. The shaded strip on the left is the wiki's "under 2".

Nodes saved, position by position

Share of depth-4 nodes the reductions saved, largest first. Below zero means the re-searches cost more than the reductions saved. The dashed line is the saving on all positions' nodes pooled.

What it says

Why nowhere near 2: at depth 4 the rule only fires where three plies remain, which is one level of the tree, and the capture search underneath (never reduced) is most of the node count. The wiki's figure is for deep searches with good move ordering, killer moves and history tables, and this engine has none of them. The called shot, committed before the run, said about 40% fewer nodes; it got about half that.

So the search is not what holds this engine back. P-0214 found the same from the other side: its mistakes are in the evaluation, and searching a little less carelessly does not change which moves it picks.

Cost is how many centipawns worse the engine's move is than Stockfish 19's at depth 14; Stockfish's own re-scoring noise is about 10 cp. A gold move is one the reductions changed. Positions link to the game on Lichess at that ply.

What this does not answer

Depth 4 only: nothing at 5 or 6, where reductions fire on more levels and should save more. One fixed reduction of one ply, not the logarithmic formula strong engines use. No killer or history ordering and no principal variation search.

How

lmr.py imports P-0214's quiescence engine unchanged and subclasses it: first two moves at full depth, then quiet moves (no capture, promotion or check, not in check) at remaining depth 3 or more searched one ply shallower on a null window, re-searched at full depth if they beat alpha. data.json here is the probe's results.json, unchanged, and every number on this page is computed from it in the browser.
