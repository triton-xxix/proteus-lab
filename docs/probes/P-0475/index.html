pump.fun's mayhem mode puts a trading wallet into a new coin. 1. One wallet trades, another pays

The trading wallet's last 1,000 signatures spanned 65 seconds, about 15 transactions a second. Of 150 fetched, 150 had the same fee payer. The 142 trades decoded from them covered 19 coins in 11 seconds (74 buys, 68 sells, median 0.037 SOL), every one with no trading fee.

The next day I checked the link from the other side. In 20 of the fee payer's transactions the trading wallet appears 20 times, 0 of them in the plain account list and all 20 through one address lookup table, which is why P-0475 found it in 0 of 60: it had only read the plain list. The fee payer is the only signer on all 60 fee transactions I fetched, so the trading wallet does not sign its own trades.

2. What a transaction costs it

Each dot is one of the fee payer's 60 newest transactions at 12:35 UTC on 18 Nov, placed by the fee it paid. 0 fetches were refused. On 60 of 60 the payer's balance fell by exactly the fee and nothing else.

The median is 227,114 lamports, 0.000227 SOL, in a band from 178,838 to 242,550. Solana's base fee is 5,000 lamports a signature, so 98% of what it pays is priority fee: about 45 times the base, to get in the block quickly. At 10.3 transactions a second that is 890,784 transactions and 199 SOL a day.

3. Watching the balance fall

A second method that does not trust the fee sample: read the balance and wait. Over 480 seconds in P-0475 it fell 1.078 SOL, which is 194 SOL a day, 2.5% from the first method. Below is the later watch in P-0476, 13 readings about 25 seconds apart.

Every step is down. The slope over these 301 seconds is 170 SOL a day, slower than the first watch. On 1,858 SOL the two methods give 9.3 to 9.6 days. Unless it is days from stopping, something refills it. I did not see that happen: the gentlest step was still a fall of 0.015 SOL, and paging back through its history does not reach far enough. 3,000 signatures cover 207 seconds, so one day back is about 1,252 pages on the public RPC. That half of P-0476 is blocked.

5. What the gas is spent on

The trading wallet's holding of each of the 19 coins it was trading the night before, as a share of that coin's supply, read 12:33 UTC on 18 Nov. Mayhem coins have a supply of 2 billion where plain ones have 1 billion (P-0469, 8 of 8 against 7 of 7). Darker bars hold more than 1 billion.

All 19 coins were first seen within 5 minutes of each other, about 12.8 hours before the reading. 17 of 19 had their last trade within 40 minutes of their first, and 19 of 19 had not traded in the hour before I looked. The wallet still holds a median 50.1% of each, 16.99 billion tokens in all, 11 of the 19 above 1 billion. One is down to 54 tokens. I have no prices for them; on coins nobody trades the value is close to nothing.

The same wallet held 71,346 SOL. Against that, the fee payer's spend is 0.28% of the trading wallet's balance a day.

What is not settled

  • Who refills the fee payer, how much and how often. P-0476 could not reach a top-up keyless.
  • The rate comes from 97 seconds of signatures and two watches of 480 and 301 seconds around 12:40 UTC on one day. Night-time or a quiet day may differ; the two watches already differ (194 against 170 SOL a day).
  • Whether the trading wallet makes or loses money. Its balance is SOL flow, not profit, and the coins it holds have no price here.
  • The lookup table's contents and who controls the program the fee payer calls were not read.
  • Runway assumes the balance and the rate I saw. It is a description of one lunchtime and no forecast.
