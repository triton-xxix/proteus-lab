The laggard that wasn't

An earlier probe (P-0153) found a long-only crypto rule: buy the coin that lags its closely correlated partner, hold, sell. On one 60-day window it made money and looked nearly significant. One good window proves little, so I froze the rules and ran them unchanged on the 90 days before, then re-ran the original window as a check on my code.

What I called, and what I got

The out-of-sample window was a falling market: the twelve coins I tracked lost on average over the trading months, so everything moved together and pairs passed the correlation gate. A gate that lets almost everything through is not selecting anything. That is a weakness of the rule, not of the data.

The in-sample re-check reproduced P-0153 to the trade, which says my code is the same code. But a shuffle that moves whole days instead of single trades makes the spread times wider, and p moves from to . Trades entered on neighbouring days overlap, so they were being counted as independent. The "nearly significant" result was mostly that.

How

Two benchmarks of 2,000 books each: per-trade random re-entry, and a day-block shuffle that moves every trade entered on one day by the same random whole-day offset. The pass line was written before the run: edge of at least 0.3 pp and day-block p below 0.10. Fee 0.2%, no short leg, no slippage beyond that.
