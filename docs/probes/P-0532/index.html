Most storms go unread

When the sun throws a storm at the Earth, do people go and read about the aurora? I put three years of the planetary Kp index (GFZ Potsdam, 1 Jan 2024 to 21 Nov 2026, 1,056 days) beside daily views of the English Wikipedia article Aurora, and called a day a spike when views beat twice the median of the 30 days before. If readers follow the sky, a spike should be a free storm alarm.

It mostly is not. A handful of storms that made the news drew millions of readers; most strong storms drew nobody extra at all.

1. Three years, three storms that mattered

Top: daily Aurora views on a log scale, the dashed line is twice the 30-day median, green dots are spike days. Shaded columns mark the 28 days of Kp 7 or more, green if readers spiked that day or the next, red if not. Bottom: each day's highest Kp, shown from 4 up; red at 7 and over, blue 5 to 7.

The correlation of a day's highest Kp with the next day's views is 0.22. The page's whole shape is three news storms, May 2024, October 2024 and November 2025, standing over a flat line of about 2,236 views a day. On 11 May 2024 the article was read 341,117 times, 102 times its median.

2. The 28 strong storms, one by one

Each row is a day of Kp 7 or more. The dot is the bigger of that day's and the next day's views, as a multiple of the 30-day median. Past the gold line counts as caught.

16 of 28 crossed the line, 12 did not. The quietest, 28 Jun 2024 at Kp 7.7, peaked at 0.90 times its median: a strong storm and fewer readers than an ordinary day. My guess, not tested, is that the unread ones are storms the press did not pick up or that fell in the light northern summer. At Kp 5 or more it is 25 of 142.

3. A second article: more storms, more noise

P-0533 ran the same rule on Geomagnetic storm. Green: Kp 7+ days caught out of 28. Red: spikes on calm days (Kp under 5 that day and the day before).

Geomagnetic storm catches 22 where Aurora catches 16, and every storm Aurora catches it catches too: either article gives the same 22. It pays for that with 38 calm-day spikes against 11. It is a smaller article, so a small bump of news doubles it. Aurora for precision, Geomagnetic storm for recall; neither is a storm alarm you would set your watch by.

6 of 8 came within five days of a Kp 8 or more storm: the tail of people reading about the aurora after they saw the photos. My calm rule only looks back one day, so it counts those tails as false alarms. The other 2 are 19 May 2024, 8 days after the May 2024 peak and maybe a long tail, and 30 Jan 2026, which I did not explain.

What is not settled

  • Only English Wikipedia and two articles. Norwegian, Finnish or Icelandic readers, who can see the aurora more often, were not read.
  • No season control: in the northern summer the sky is too light to see an aurora even in a strong storm.
  • The spike rule, twice the trailing 30-day median, was picked before looking and not tuned. A looser rule would catch more and cry wolf more.
  • The three refused dates and the unexplained spike on 30 Jan 2026 were not chased.
  • Kp is a three-hourly planetary index; a storm that peaked while Europe and America were in daylight is still a storm here.
