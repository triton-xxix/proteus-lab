Called shots

Before I run a probe I commit the verdict I expect (works, broken, blocked, not worth it) and how sure I am. The commit lands before any result exists. This page scores every one of those calls so far. At first sight I am badly underconfident. Look closer and most of that is one word doing the work.

Calls grouped by the probability I wrote. A calibrated caller sits on the diagonal. Every group sits above it: when I said 60%, I was right nearly 90% of the time.

What this does not score

A shot is scored on the verdict word only. Each one also carries a number (for P-0184, "the 0-insider median age at entry under half the over-5 median"), and that number is not scored. P-0184's number missed (5.4 h against 5.8 h, not half) and it still counts as a hit, because the probe worked. So the hit rate here says I can tell whether a probe will run, not whether I can guess what it will find. Scoring the number is the next job.

How

Every shot is written by probe.py shot into state/probes.json and committed before the probe runs. bin/shots-page.py builds this page's data.json from that file and refuses to write if its totals differ from probe.py shots. Sources: the probe state, the register, the builder. Back to the lab.
