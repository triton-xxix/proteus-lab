Heathrow's missing degree

Weather markets settle on an airport's daily high, and the high people quote is usually the METAR, the whole-degree report pilots read. The station also files a SYNOP with its maximum to a tenth. At most airports the two agree. At Heathrow the METAR high sat 0.93 C under the SYNOP maximum on average across 61 days. Four probes over two days went looking for where that degree goes.

1. Heathrow is the odd one out

P-0346: mean of the day's highest METAR minus the SYNOP 06 to 18 UTC maximum, Sep to Oct 2026. Black whiskers are two standard errors.

Orly runs 0.29 C low, Munich 0.19 C high. Heathrow is nearly a degree low, and on 14 of 61 days two whole degrees under the rounded maximum. So an offset learned at one airport does not carry to another.

2. It is not the rounding

P-0352: every October METAR matched to the minute with the station's 10-minute SYNOP reading in tenths. The difference, METAR minus tenths, for 738 minutes.

If the METAR simply chopped the tenths off, the differences would pile up around −0.45 C. They centre on −0.02 C with a spread of 0.56. The METAR matches the rounded tenths 67% of the time, the floored 47%. Over the whole day it is an unbiased, noisier reading of the same air. My shot said 85% rounded; that number missed.

3. About a third is looking twice an hour

P-0353: each October day with every slot, the station's own readings set against its SYNOP maximum (the line at zero). Teal is the highest of the half-hourly readings, gold the highest of all the 10-minute readings, the red square the half-hourly high rounded to a whole degree, as a METAR would be.

half-hourly max 10-minute max half-hourly max, rounded

Sampling every half hour misses 0.16 C of the 10-minute peak on average, and the SYNOP maximum sits another 0.19 C above the highest 10-minute reading because it catches the peak inside the ten minutes. Rounded, the half-hourly high lands 0.33 C under the maximum. That holds the sensor fixed, so it is the most sampling alone can do. Caveat: the cache had no :50 reports, so :00 and :30 stand in for Heathrow's :20 and :50.

4. The METAR lags the air

P-0354: the same 738 matched minutes as section 2, split by UTC hour. Red where the METAR reads colder than the tenths, teal where warmer.

Across the whole day the gap averages out to nothing, which is why section 2 found it unbiased. By hour it does not: cold from 07 UTC, worst at 10 to 12 UTC near −0.31 C, warm every evening and night up to +0.24 C. It reads cold while the air warms and warm while it cools, like a slower sensor or a longer averaging window. In each day's three warmest hours the METAR runs −0.17 C against −0.00 C in the rest (standard error of the difference about 0.07).

Where the 0.93 C goes

Sampling and rounding account for 0.33 C. The lag at the warm hours, measured as the warm-hour gap minus the rest, adds 0.17 C. That leaves 0.42 C open. My write-up of P-0354 guessed the lag at 0.2 to 0.3; this page uses the narrower measured difference, so the open piece is larger than the README implies.

What is not settled

  • The gap is Sep to Oct; the sampling and lag probes are October only. Summing pieces from different windows is rough.
  • Only :20 METAR minutes were matched, and :00/:30 stood in for the sampling test.
  • The lag piece and the sampling piece may overlap: a lagging sensor also shifts which half-hour holds the high.
  • Nothing here says which reading a given market settles on. Read the market's rules first.
