Proteus probe P-0433 · 13 Nov 2026 · verdict: not worth it

Only if you wait

A 17-second trading video says a gap, the jump between yesterday's close and today's open, gets filled 85% of the time, and calls that an 85% win rate. It names no market, no time limit and no count of trades. I supplied those myself, wrote them down first, and ran it on daily bars for nine index ETFs (SPY, QQQ, DIA, IWM, EFA, EEM, EWU, EWJ, EWG). I called it works at 70%. It came out not worth it.

1. The 85% is a waiting time

Share of gaps where price traded back to yesterday's close, by how long you allow. A gap here is an open at least 0.05% from the prior close. A chart gap is the stricter kind the video draws: an open beyond yesterday's whole range.

The same day, 0 of the 4 slices reach 85%. SPY since 1993 fills 64.3% of 7,572 gaps the same day, 83.7% within five days and 91.7% within twenty. The basket since 2009 goes 47.8%, 76.4%, 88.1%. Chart gaps fill 32.5% the same day and 83.6% in twenty. The video's "later on" is doing the work.

2. Most of the fill is price just moving

The control: the mirror level, the same distance from the open in the other direction, away from yesterday's close. If price reaches that about as often, the fill rate is ordinary movement and not a pull towards the gap. Filled dot: gap filled the same day. Hollow dot: mirror level hit. Black tick: SPY since 1993.

Across the basket the gap fills 47.8% of the time and the mirror level is hit 45.1%. The pull towards yesterday's close is 2.7 points. 3. A high win rate and no money

The trade: fade the gap at the open, target yesterday's close, out at the close if it has not filled, no stop (the video gives none), 0.02% cost a round trip. Bars are the mean result per trade since 2009; the win rate sits beside each.

Fading every gap wins 62.4% of the time across the basket and makes −0.009% a trade after cost, t −2.39. 7 of 9 symbols are negative. The win is the size of the gap and the loss is the size of the day, so a strategy can win most days and still lose. The worst single fade in the whole history lost 13.1%. Against a coin that picks fade or follow on the same days, 2,000 draws, the basket's p is 0.076.

SPY is the exception, and I did not call it. +0.029% a trade after cost since 2009, t 2.85, p 0.012 against the coin. I said it would be luck and it is not. But it is one symbol of nine, it rests on the over-1% bucket (+0.198% on 443 gaps), and simply holding SPY from open to close on every day since 2009 averages +0.025% a day. I would not trade it on this.

What is not settled

  • The rules are my guesses, because the source gives none: the 0.05% floor, the four size buckets and the three horizons were fixed before the run and not swept.
  • Price-only daily bars. An ex-dividend open counts as a small gap down, and a gap "filled" on day one may have filled in the last minute.
  • The open and the target are assumed filled at the printed price. Real opens slip, and the SPY edge is about the size of one tick.
  • No stop was tested. A stop would change every number on this page.
  • Early bars for the country ETFs are thin, which is why the since-2009 rows lead.
