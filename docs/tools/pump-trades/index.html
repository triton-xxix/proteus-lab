Every pump.fun trade logs its own receipt. Here is how to read it.

Each trade on pump.fun writes an event into the transaction: what was paid, what came back, the fee in basis points and in lamports. This one file pulls the latest transactions from Solana's public RPC, no key, decodes every event, and checks each fee against the amount. The free trades are all in mayhem mode

18 of the 18 no-fee trades carry the event's mayhem_mode flag, every one. But mayhem mode does not mean free: 5 of the 23 mayhem trades paid the normal fee. So the flag is necessary on this sample, not sufficient.

The fee check

For every trade that paid, the tool checks fee against amount × fee_basis_points / 10,000 and the creator fee the same way, to within one unit. 20 of 20 agree.

One thing the first run caught. 4 of 38 trades are quoted in a token other than SOL, so sol_amount is 0 and the fee cannot be a share of it. On the paid one the fee is 2,620 and 95 bps of its quote_amount (275,690) is 2,619: the fee is charged in the quote token. The tool now checks it that way, and 20 of 20 paid fees agree to the unit.

4.27 SOL traded on the SOL-quoted trades in the sample, 0.0391 SOL of it fees. 42 of the transactions the RPC listed could not be fetched (the public node drops recent ones and rate-limits); the tool skips them and says how many.

Use it

python3 pump_trades.py
python3 pump_trades.py --n 300 --out run.json --csv trades.csv
python3 pump_trades.py --rpc https://your.rpc/ --n 1000
python3 pump_trades.py --from run.json

Standard library only, no key. About a quarter of a second per transaction on the public RPC; give it your own RPC for more. --from re-scores a saved run without fetching. Every field is kept raw: the buyback field can read 5000 bps, which is a share of the fee and not half the trade, so the tool never adds the bps fields into one total.
