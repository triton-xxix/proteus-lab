A million petition signatures that belong to no seat

Every UK Parliament petition publishes two counts that should match: how many signatures came from the UK, and how many landed in each of the 650 constituencies. They do not. On the 400 most-signed petitions of the 2019-24 Parliament, 43,124,436 UK signatures were counted and 1,018,173 of them (2.36%) sit in no constituency at all. This one file measures that gap for any petition, keyless.

It is a step in early 2022, not a slope

Share of UK signatures in no seat, by the month the petition opened. Every month before December 2021 runs 2.3% to 3.9%. December 1.83%, January 1.51%, February 2022 0.62%, and every month after sits between 0.47% and 1.03%.

Size does nothing

Split by how big the petition got, the hole stays the same inside each period. Petitions opened in the lockdown months carry 3.7 to 4.6 times the 2023-24 share in every size band.

What it is not

Not a slow climb with traffic, which was my first guess (P-0516) and which this run's flat size bands rule out. Not the 2017-19 Parliament either: there the 40 most-signed show 1.68% against 2.64% for 2019-24. A step points to a change in how signatures were matched to seats, made around January to February 2022. The site's public code history does not name it (P-0519), so the cause is still open. Not one of the 399 petitions read had more signatures placed than counted.

Use it

python3 seatless.py --ids 300412 241584
python3 seatless.py --parliament 4 --top 400 --floor 10000
python3 seatless.py --parliament 3 --top 100 --out run.json --csv rows.csv
python3 seatless.py --from run.json

Standard library only, no key. A named petition is looked up in the archive first, then among open ones. About two petitions a second with the default spacing. --from re-scores a saved run without fetching; the month histogram prints in the terminal.
