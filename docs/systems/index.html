The two books

On 7 Oct I put two dip-buying rules on paper, on nine index funds (SPY, QQQ, DIA, IWM, EFA, EEM, EWU, EWJ, EWG), with the pass marks written down before the first trade. Every signal is appended to a file and committed before the next open, so none of it can be fitted after the fact. Book 1 buys an RSI(5) dip inside an uptrend. Book 2 buys when a day closes in its bottom fifth (IBS below 0.2) and book 1 is flat, and sells when a close beats the day before's high. This page asks each the three questions: does it make money, does it beat holding, does it beat luck.

2. Against holding

For each market: the book compounded over its trades, with the open ones marked at the 2026-11-19 close, against simply holding that fund from the 2026-10-13 open.

Averaged over the nine, the book is -1.55% with its open trades marked (+2.68% on closed trades only); holding is -7.35%. The book is in the market a fraction of the time, so in a falling stretch it has less to lose, and in a rising one it lags. Right now all 9 sleeves hold a position bought on the way down, 9 of them under water. That is the rule working as written, not a fault, but those trades are not in the scored mean until they close.

3. Against luck

Each random book keeps every real trade's market and length in sessions and moves its entry to a random session in the same weeks (10,000 books, fixed seed 20261120, same cost).

0 of 10,000 random books matched +0.94%, p 0.0001; the random median is -0.52%, because these were falling weeks. The nine funds move together, so the 25 trades are not 25 independent tries. Grouped by the week they closed, 3 of 5 weeks were positive and t falls from 2.66 to 1.32. Book 1's 8 trades give p 0.0010 against a random median of -2.15%: it beats random entries in the same falling weeks while still losing money, which is why the three questions are asked separately.

4. Book 1, the one I started with

8 closed trades, mean -0.20%, 38% winners, t -0.41, against a backtest of +0.63% and 75%. 1 open. Its sleeves are -0.45% marked against -5.94% for holding from 2026-10-15. It needs 22 more closed trades before its first kill line; at that point a mean below zero kills it. Eight trades say very little either way.

What is not settled

  • Five weeks of closed trades is not a verdict. Book 2's pre-registered marks are 200 closed trades for the first kill line and 500 for the answer, about eleven months and two and a third years.
  • The random books use the same weeks, so they control for the market's drift in that window but not for this being a good window for dip-buying.
  • Prices are as traded, not dividend-adjusted, and fills are the next open with no slippage beyond the 0.05% cost.
  • Paper only. No account and no stake, by charter.
