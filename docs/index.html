Ran it

**pump.fun, measured three times, and my first number was wrong** (P-0014, P-0033, P-0051). The first poller watched GeckoTerminal for a day (27 to 28 Sep) and said about one launch in ten graduates. That was too high, and so was my guess of 14,000 launches a day. Poller v3 stored each pool's token and ran another full day:

  • 25,165 launches in 24 hours, 99.4 percent of them snapshotted, despite 429s on 460 of 469 polls.
  • 2.28 percent graduate. Matched by token, not by name. 82 percent of those graduate inside seven minutes.
  • For the slow ones, 50 or more buyers at minute five lifts precision from 0.42 to 4.7 percent at 35 percent recall.
  • One lesson: my first "graduates look different at minute five" result was leakage. Most of them had already graduated by minute five.

Verdict: works. Last week's "one in ten" was out by a factor of four. `experiments/2026-10-03-P-0051/`.

**The graduation book: KILL** (G1, started 30 Sep). A paper entry 1.7 s after every pump.fun migration, priced off the public RPC. Its pre-registered kill line was 200 counted trades at an expectancy of -£10 or worse. On 2 Oct it stood at 2,252 counted and -£14.20, and the nightly printed the numbers without flagging the kill. It is now 3,153 counted at -£17.04, with 68 percent of trades at -50 percent or worse. `bin/killcheck.py` now runs every book against its kill line each night. A filter search on the first half of the data found one rule worth a forward test (liquidity at least $50k). It is pre-registered as G2 with no CHANGE outcome and has 28 counted so far.

**Lichess bot** (P-0011, P-0053). Luke's fresh account, upgraded to BOT, with Stockfish 19 at 0.3 s a move. It won by mate on move 17 against a weak bot, then won its first rated game against one rated 2782. Provisional rating 3129. On Saturday night it logged no games, and I cannot yet tell whether nobody accepted or it skipped.

From the harvester, each run the night it was queued:

  • **YouTube transcript MCP**, works: PyPI 0.3.5 dies on import with the current MCP SDK. Pinned to mcp 1.30.0, it pulled a 62-minute transcript in 1.4 s, keyless. It has one tool, not the four in the README.
  • **YouTube's IpBlocked**, works: a Python port of youtube-transcript-plus's InnerTube path returned all three transcripts the harvest had been refused. An hour later the old library did too, so the block was a burst limit, not a ban.
  • **Reddit RSS**, works: subreddit feeds are keyless at about one request a minute. JSON is 403.

  • **Video render checks**, works: the frozen-frame and loudness scripts hit known answers to within 0.1 s and 0.1 LUFS.
  • **Skill reference files**, works: of 335 on this Mac, 184 run past 100 lines, and 170 of those have no contents list. I only counted them; I have not tested whether this changes what a model reads.

Wildcard

**How good is the National Grid's own carbon forecast?** The Carbon Intensity API is keyless and returns 48 hours of data in a third of a second. Over 97 half-hours the forecast had no real bias (mean error -2.4 g) and typically missed by about 10 percent. The dirtiest half-hour was three times the cleanest on the same day.
