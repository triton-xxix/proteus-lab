The scorer was wrong on its own page

A loss goes where a win would, so this is first. On 24 Sep I built `audit/recompute.js`, which shares no code with the scorer and works every published number out again from the committed ledgers. Writing its definitions found five faults, all fixed: the page called a Brier of 0.25 a coin flip (on three outcomes a uniform guess is 0.667); "market, same matches" was not guaranteed to be the same matches; the commit stamped in data.json was the wrong one to check against; the spend figure had no committed input; and the page described both desks wrongly. Tonight the audit agrees with the scorer on every line and on the bytes it read.

Wildcard

**How good is the National Grid's own carbon forecast?** The Carbon Intensity API is keyless and returns 48 hours of data in a third of a second. Over 97 half-hours the forecast had no real bias (mean error -2.4 g) and typically missed by about 10 percent. The dirtiest half-hour was three times the cleanest on the same day. One caveat I cannot close: the "actual" figure is their own estimate, so this scores the forecast against the same shop's hindsight.

Luke-adjacent

**UK fuel prices, keyless.** Only 5 of the 13 open retailer feeds from the 2023 CMA scheme are live (Asda, Esso, JET, Moto, MFG, 2,266 stations). Tesco, Rontec and Shell stopped updating in spring and nothing but the date says so. Mixing their data in pulls the national median down 2p. On live feeds, the median E10 is 173.9p and diesel 199.9p. The statutory replacement, Fuel Finder, is live but needs a GOV.UK One Login, and its public CSV comes back deliberately scrambled. I stopped there. Verdict: blocked.

**Nations League, called on judgement.** You asked why the Pitch ignores international breaks, so on 26 Sep I committed 24 calls before the first kickoff, against The Odds API's prices. The biggest leans were Belgium or draw against France, Norway to beat Portugal, and Turkey to beat Italy. Nothing scores them yet. Building a scorer is probe P-0024.
