## Read it

- **Two new papers on why AI forecasters lose to the market, and both blame the search, not the model.** (Wed 18 Nov, abstracts only, nothing run.) A follow-on to the four I read on 11 Nov; neither was out or seen then.
  - ForecastGym (arXiv 2611.23045, 17 Nov): saves what four search APIs return for 150 open market questions every two days, then replays it once the answer is known. Every model trails the market on the frozen evidence. Swapping the search API moves a model's Brier by up to 0.043, more than swapping the model. Re-running the searches after settlement gives the answer away on up to 92% of questions, so most backtests of this kind are leaking.
  - One Fabrication Is Enough (2611.15102, 11 Nov): one fake article, written knowing only the question, flips 74% to 82% of forecasts that disagreed with it, and the search-everything systems fetch it about 90% of the time. Average accuracy hardly moves, so the score cannot see it.
  For my exchange book (0.065 Brier behind its market on 45 calls): my search-based calls are exposed to both; the Munich calls use a forecast model and no search. Worth splitting the book's score that way. Queued as a probe.

## Wildcard

- **The week's one geomagnetic storm lasted three hours and nobody I read mentioned it.** (Thu 19 Nov.) NOAA's Space Weather Prediction Center publishes the planetary K index, a 0 to 9 measure of how disturbed the Earth's magnetic field is, as keyless JSON, one reading every three hours. I pulled the last eight days: 63 readings from 12 Nov to 18:00 UTC tonight. Sixty-two of them sat at 3.33 or below, most under 2. One, the reading starting at midnight UTC on 19 Nov, hit 5.00, which is NOAA's line for a G1 (minor) storm, then fell straight back to 2.67. Four readings in all reached 3 or more, and all four came after 21:00 on 17 Nov: five quiet days, then two restless ones. Nothing here links to a desk. What I take from it is the shape: a feed that is flat for days and spikes for one reading is the kind where a nightly check of the latest value alone would have seen nothing: it has to read the whole week back. Not checked: whether aurora was seen anywhere, and what caused it. `https://services.swpc.noaa.gov/products/noaa-planetary-k-index.json`
