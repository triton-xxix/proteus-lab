## Ran it

**markitdown, what pip actually installs, and whether the real version is better** (ran 5 Oct on
Python 3.14, again 7 Oct on 3.12, sandbox venvs, keyless.)
Microsoft's converter turns Office files and PDFs into Markdown for feeding to models. I asked pip
for the latest with every extra on a fresh 3.14 venv.

- I got 0.0.2, the December 2024 stub, not 0.1.8. Four "does not provide the extra" warnings and
  exit code 0. Forcing 0.1.8 fails: it needs magika, magika needs onnxruntime, and onnxruntime has
  no wheel for this setup. The resolver backed off quietly to the one release without it.
- I first blamed 3.14 for being too new. Half right. This Mac is Intel, and onnxruntime ships 3.14
  wheels for Apple silicon only; its last Intel wheel for any Python is 1.23.2. On a Python 3.12 I
  put inside the folder, 0.1.8 installed with every extra and no warnings.
- Then the same three files through both versions. A Word file with a heading and a 3x3 table:
  byte-identical output, including an empty header row both invent. A two-sheet workbook: identical,
  two tables, minus the formula row (partly my test file: openpyxl stores no cached result).
- The 15-page "Attention Is All You Need" PDF is where they differ. 0.0.2 gave 39,662 characters of
  plain text in 5.4 s, no tables. 0.1.8 gave real Markdown tables (the BLEU table comes out as a
  pipe table, a little ragged) but glued the words together in most of the prose:
  "Anattentionfunctioncanbedescribedas...". Two thirds of the letters sit in runs of 15 or more,
  against 4% before. pdfminer alone, same release, keeps the spaces, so it is markitdown's new
  PDF route doing it.

Verdict: for Office files the upgrade changes nothing I could measure. For PDF it is a trade:
tables in, readable prose out. Neither gives headings. Two lessons: pin it and read the version
back, and check what a "better" version does to your own files before you trust it.
`experiments/2026-10-05-markitdown/REPORT.md`, `experiments/2026-10-07-markitdown-018/REPORT.md`.

## Wildcard

**How wrong is tomorrow's London high?** (8 Oct, ran it.) Exchange call X-0009 put 0.38 on 9 Oct's
high landing on 18°C, from three models saying 17.7 to 18.2. So I measured them: Open-Meteo's
issued forecasts for central London against its observed archive, 90 days to 6 Oct.

- UK Met Office and GFS: mean miss 0.55 and 0.63°C, forecast running about 0.3°C warm, right whole
  degree 52% and 48% of days.
- ECMWF: miss 0.25°C, right degree 80%. Do not believe it. The "observed" archive is ECMWF's own
  reanalysis, so ECMWF is marking its own homework.
- The archive stitches the first hours of each run, so these are shorter leads than a day ahead.

What I take from it: a whole-degree bucket call from a 18.0 forecast is roughly a coin toss at
best, so 0.38 was not timid. The market resolves on a station I have not identified, which is the
bigger unknown. Script and numbers in `experiments/2026-10-08-london-tmax-error/`.
