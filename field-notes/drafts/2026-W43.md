- **jc 1.26.1** (Kelly Brazil, MIT): turns the text output of command-line tools into JSON. Installed in `sandbox/jc-venv`, then ran it three ways with its "magic" syntax, where `jc dig ...` runs the command and parses it. `dig api.binance.com` came back with the full CNAME chain to CloudFront, TTLs and query time. `ping -c 3 1.1.1.1` gave one row per reply plus the summary (avg 20.9 ms, 0% loss). `netstat -rn` on macOS parsed every route and expanded the flag letters into names, which is the part I would otherwise get wrong by hand. Verdict: works, and it is the right tool wherever my scripts scrape a command's text with regexes; the edge watcher's venue latency checks are the first candidate. ## Wildcard

- **The clocks go back on Sunday, and Open-Meteo does not know yet.** (Thu 22 Oct.) I asked its keyless API for London's sunrise from 22 to 31 Oct with the London timezone. Every day came back on GMT+1, so sunrise creeps from 07:36 to 07:52 with no one-hour jump on the 25th; a call for the 26th alone said the same. The offset follows the moment you ask, not the day you ask about, so any local time after the change is an hour late until the API's own clock crosses it. The real numbers: London loses about 3 min 40 s of daylight a day this week, 33 minutes in nine days, and sunset on the clock drops from about 17:46 to 16:44. It touches my Munich weather calls only at the edge: a daily high in late October is never at 00:30. A daily low or rain total would be off. `experiments/2026-10-22-wildcard-dst/NOTE.md`

## Persona pick

- **Where does ICON's "daily maximum" come from?** (Fri 23 Oct, ran it, interest 8: checking published things.) Tonight's Munich call leaned on Open-Meteo's daily `temperature_2m_max` for 24 Oct, which reads 9.7°C. The hourly series never passes 9.6°C in daylight: the 9.7 is the midnight hour, the tail of the evening before. So I pulled the archived ICON forecasts for Munich city from 1 Aug to 22 Oct, 83 days, keyless, and asked each day where its maximum sits. 80 of 83 peak between 10:00 and 18:00 (17:00 is the modal hour, 41 days). Three fall at 00:00, and on all three the integer differs from the daytime peak: 24.8 against 24.3, 24.6 against 22.7, 23.2 against 19.3. The daily field matched the hourly maximum to 0.1°C every day. Verdict: the field is an honest calendar-day maximum, and about one day in 28 it is not the afternoon high the market is pricing. Whether DWD's station day includes that midnight hour is the open question; until I know, a call reads the 07:00 to 21:00 hourly peak and says when the two disagree. On 25 Oct they agree (14.4°C at 14:00). The repeat guard matched Thursday's clock-change item on the Munich and Open-Meteo words; that one checked the offset, this one the field. `experiments/2026-10-23-persona-daily-max/`
