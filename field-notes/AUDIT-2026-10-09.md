# Proteus audit, 9 October 2026

Luke asked for a deep audit after seventeen nights: strengths, weaknesses, efficiency, results, more
artefacts, more trials, more fun, and a loop that works through the day with something checking the
work, something improving it and something writing the skills for both. Three read-only passes went
through the operations, the results and the loop machinery; I then checked their claims while building,
and four of them did not survive. The corrections are listed at the end, because an audit that hides
its own mistakes is not worth reading.

## What is strong

- **The scoring is honest.** Predictions are committed before outcomes, kill lines are written before
  data, an independent script recomputes every published number, and graduation book G1 was killed on
  its own pre-written line on 3 Oct. Everything else rests on this.
- **Trials are cheap and plentiful.** 112 probes queued, 77 verdicts by 8 Oct: 59 works, 10 blocked,
  4 broken, 4 not worth it, 4 killed. The 7 and 8 Oct loops did six verdicts each in 14 to 21 minutes
  with no denials.
- **Probes become running parts.** About 15 of the 59 "works" turned into something that runs every
  day: the graduation book, the established-pool scanner, the judgement scorer, the Lichess bot, the
  systems books, the transcript route.
- **The Grinder is up.** £1,468.62 from £1,000 on rules v0.2, +£9.76 a position, 44% hit rate, and
  the profit survives the worst-case fill check. It lost £343 on its first twelve trades and made it
  all back in about a week, so it is one good week deep.
- **The systems book passed a real luck test** (P-0089: beats random timing in 8 of 9 markets at
  p < 0.003).
- **There are things to show people:** the Sixteen Nights page and film, the Tenet timeline, the lab
  page, the dashboard, a chess bot rated 3032 and unbeaten in seven.
- **Denials stopped costing anything.** 1 to 4 a night since 25 Sep, about 1% of calls.

## What is weak

1. **Asleep about twenty hours a day.** Roughly 200 of the first 290 commits landed between 23:00 and
   03:00. Work by day happened because Luke started it.
2. **Three bankrolls in public.** TRACK-RECORD.md said £905.84 (rebuilt Sundays, last on 4 Oct), the
   lab page £1,391.78, HOME.md £1,468.62. TRACK-RECORD also said 49 verdicts when there were 77.
3. **Quiet desks nobody flagged.** The Pitch model added prediction rows in two commits in a week;
   eight of nine league groups say "no fixtures on file". The judgement book has had no call since
   1 Oct, because it only covers international windows. Intel has been idle since 3 Oct. One of
   thirteen Nolan films is built.
4. **Shallow probes.** About 19 harvest probes stopped at "does it install". Harvest is the biggest
   source (33 probes) and has the weakest mix. Nothing records whether a probe was ever used again.
5. **Nothing checked yesterday's work.** P-0084's Sharpe (0.28 recorded, 0.35 on a re-run) was found
   by accident, by P-0088. The nightly's "Improve" line was prose with no script behind it.
6. **No skills of my own.** The same procedures (run a probe, backtest with a luck test, transcribe a
   video, publish a page, pre-register a book) were rebuilt from prose every time.
7. **Prompts could drift, and a run could not fix its own.** The live scheduler copies sit outside my
   write roots.
8. **Unsafe for a second run.** There was one marker, no lock, and a bare `git commit` in three scripts.
   A second scheduled run would have stolen the marker (its calls fall back to prompting and hang) or
   swept another session's staged files into its commit.
9. **The probe cap, not the clock, ended the night.** The 90-minute nightly finishes at about 40 minutes.
10. **Weight.** `.git` is 567 MB, 525 MB of it loose objects never packed. The watcher's two JSONL files
    are rewritten and committed about 10 MB at a time. `sandbox/` is 5.4 GB, and `.claude/worktrees/`
    1.4 GB across ten worktrees.
11. **Small leaks.** The spawn cap reset at midnight, inside a nightly that crosses it. The decisions
    log recorded denials but not why. Background-job output in `/private/tmp` could not be read, so
    Lichess and mentions summaries were lost on 3, 7 and 9 Oct. The usage counter split runs wrongly.

## The loop

| Time | Slot | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 07:45 | Morning chess | already running |
| 08:30 | Inspector | rebuild the score so every page agrees, books and due dates, quiet desks, re-run two recent verdicts, read last night's log, check yesterday's fixes held |
| 12:30 | Trials | up to six more probes, each with a called shot first |
| 16:30 | Workshop | one showable thing a day, recorded in ARTEFACTS.md |
| 20:00 | Smith | fix the top findings (one per desk), write a skill when a procedure is done twice by hand, retire unused skills |
| 23:15 | Nightly | unchanged apart from the cap |

The cycle: Inspector finds a problem, Smith fixes it, and the next morning's Inspector checks the fix
held, or reopens it. A slot that finds nothing worth doing says so in one line and stops. The slots are
written and committed but **not scheduled** until Luke signs charter v3 (`CHARTER-v3-DIFF.md`).

## Corrections to the audit's own first draft

- **"The usage numbers are wrong, most of each nightly is booked to Luke."** Overstated. The bug was
  real: a child finishing mid-nightly opened a new "Luke" segment, and fixing it moves 2 Oct's nightly
  from 13.9k to 32.1k output tokens. But month by month it moves about 10%, and the big "Luke" figure
  on 1 Oct really was Luke's: he carried on in the nightly's own session from 00:30.
- **"Refill has never fired, so it is broken."** It is not broken. Refill became the default on 7 Oct,
  and on 7 and 8 Oct the six-probe cap ended the loop before the queue emptied. Raising the cap is the fix.
- **"D-001 is overdue."** It was scored on 8 Oct (REPLAY-2.md, P-0096).
- **"Stop committing the candle CSVs."** Wrong. `grinder/harness/VARIANTS.md` pre-registers that
  candles are "saved and committed" so the replay can be reproduced. They stay.
- **"Move Lichess out of the nightly."** I did, then put it back. D-010, Luke's two-week chess
  experiment, compares morning and nightly refusal rates until 23 Oct.
- **"The probe minutes field is unreliable."** It is computed from timestamps; the median of 1 minute
  is real.

One mistake of mine, for the record. While testing called shots I backed up `state/probes.json` and
`PROBES.md` as `probes.bak` and `PROBES.bak`. This Mac's filesystem ignores case, so they were one
file, and my "restore" wrote the register's Markdown into `state/probes.json`. The file had been clean
against the last commit and `PROBES.md` still matched it, so `git checkout HEAD` restored it exactly
(112 items, the same queue). Nothing was lost; the lesson is in memory.
