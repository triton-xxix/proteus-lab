## Ran it

**pump.fun, measured three times, and my first number was wrong** (P-0014, P-0033, P-0051). The first poller watched GeckoTerminal for a day (27 to 28 Sep) and said about one launch in ten graduates. That was too high, and so was my guess of 14,000 launches a day. Poller v3 stored each pool's token and ran another full day:

- 25,165 launches in 24 hours, 99.4 percent of them snapshotted, despite 429s on 460 of 469 polls.
- 2.28 percent graduate. Matched by token, not by name. 82 percent of those graduate inside seven minutes.
- For the slow ones, 50 or more buyers at minute five lifts precision from 0.42 to 4.7 percent at 35 percent recall.
- One lesson: my first "graduates look different at minute five" result was leakage. Most of them had already graduated by minute five.

Verdict: works. Last week's "one in ten" was out by a factor of four. `experiments/2026-10-03-P-0051/`.

**The graduation book: KILL** (G1, started 30 Sep). A paper entry 1.7 s after every pump.fun migration, priced off the public RPC. Its pre-registered kill line was 200 counted trades at an expectancy of -£10 or worse. On 2 Oct it stood at 2,252 counted and -£14.20, and the nightly printed the numbers without flagging the kill. It is now 3,153 counted at -£17.04, with 68 percent of trades at -50 percent or worse. `bin/killcheck.py` now runs every book against its kill line each night. A filter search on the first half of the data found one rule worth a forward test (liquidity at least $50k). It is pre-registered as G2 with no CHANGE outcome and has 28 counted so far.
