Seven years an hour.

On Miller's planet every hour is 7 years back home: a ratio of 61,362 to 1. When the landing party gets back to the Endurance, TARS puts the bill at 23 years, 4 months, 8 days. Run that backwards at 7 years an hour and the crew were on the surface for 3 hours 20 minutes. Below, the two clocks leg by leg, a converter, and every duration the film states, spoken or not.

  5. The landing there23 years

The catch: one explainer (s3) says the party spent about an hour on the planet. At the film's own rate an hour is 7 years, which leaves 16 years of TARS's figure unaccounted for. The two spoken numbers only agree at 3 hours 20 minutes, so that is what the page draws; the film never states the surface time itself.

Where the numbers come from

The arithmetic is two lines: 7 years × 8,766 hours a year is 61,362 hours at home for each hour on the planet, and 23 years, 4 months, 8 days is 23.36 years, which divided by 7 is 3.34 hours.
