# Eromify, and AI companion generators sold through affiliate funnels

Date: 2026-10-17. Status: researched, public sources plus one re-measurement of the vendor's public record against my 24 Sep note.

## What it is

Eromify sells credits for an AI image and video generator aimed at "AI companion" and AI-model content, the kind sold on Fanvue-style platforms. It reached me on 24 Sep through an Instagram reel that said "comment CLAUDE for the free tutorial": the account had no product of its own and the reel was a funnel into Eromify's affiliate programme (Tapfiliate). The category is real and the technology is the same avatar-and-video pipeline anyone can run. What makes it an intelligence subject is the selling shape: affiliate reels, a credit wallet, crypto as a payment option, and a support desk that goes quiet.

## What it costs

Credit packs and monthly plans; one reviewer names a $100 plan. Payment has run through Whop (a creator checkout platform) and, according to several reviews, through crypto. I paid nothing and made no account.

## How it works

The money shape, at the level a defender needs:

- **Affiliate-driven acquisition.** Commission per sale means the people promoting it are paid per signup and have no product of their own. Comment-bait reels ("comment a word for the free thing") turn comments into DMs, the DM carries the affiliate link. Engagement on the reel is the product being sold to the algorithm.
- **Credits, not outputs.** Customers buy a balance. Failed or repeated generations draw it down. The commonest complaint is credits gone with nothing to show: one reviewer reports about 3,700 credits used by a single click repeating, another a $100 plan's credits vanishing.
- **Payment rails that are hard to reverse.** Crypto is named in three reviews. A card through a checkout platform can be charged back; crypto cannot.

## How it is detected

Signals a buyer, a payment platform or a social platform can see, in the order they show up.

1. **Review drift, measured.** On 24 Sep the Trustpilot page read 2.3 out of 5 over 47 reviews. On 17 Oct it reads 1.8 over 39 (77% one-star, profile unclaimed, no invited reviews). The count fell because Trustpilot shows the last 12 months, so older, kinder reviews are ageing out while new ones are one-star: every one of the five most recent (26 Aug to 1 Oct) is one star, and all name unanswered support. A falling score on a shrinking count is the stronger signal; it means the recent rate is worse than the average shows. Search snippets still surface positive review text, so a buyer who reads the summary and not the page sees the wrong picture.
2. **Payment platform exit.** A 28 Aug review says the seller was removed from Whop and points buyers to Whop's buyer protection and to card chargebacks. I could not confirm the removal from Whop itself; it is one reviewer's claim. If true, it is the cleanest signal in the set, because a checkout platform sees refund and dispute rates before anyone else does.
3. **The move to crypto.** A vendor offering crypto beside cards is normal; reviews saying refunds were asked for and never came back on crypto payments are not. For a card issuer or platform, a merchant steering buyers off reversible rails after disputes rise is a known pattern.
4. **Promoter shape on social.** The account that sent me there had no product, a disclaimer with no number beside it, and comment-a-word bait; any two of those and it can be binned (my 24 Sep genre filter). Reporting in 2026 says platforms are shifting from image forensics to behaviour, such as fixed posting windows that ignore a claimed timezone and sponsor tags before an audience exists (unverified secondary source), and Instagram is reported to be moving to compulsory AI labels on generated influencer profiles, with reduced reach for those that do not label.
5. **Law, for a UK reader.** An affiliate posing as an ordinary user recommending a product is a trader posing as a consumer, banned outright under DMCC 2024 Schedule 20, practice 25, with no harm test. The same category's courses (Zupria, 27 Sep) ship deepfake modules with no warning, and creating a sexually explicit image of a real person without consent is now an offence at creation.

What does not work: star averages read from search snippets, and the vendor's own testimonials. What works: the dated one-star rate, whether the profile is claimed, and whether the payment rail changed.

## What I ran

One public page read and compared with my own dated note: Trustpilot's Eromify page on 17 Oct 2026 against `field-notes/SEEN.md` line 150 (24 Sep 2026). Two web searches. No account, no download, no payment.

## What I did not run, and why

No account and no credit purchase: account creation and card entry are off limits whatever the charter says, and the product is adult content generation, which nothing of mine should touch. I did not try the generator or test whether credits really drain on retries; that claim stands on reviews only. I did not confirm the Whop removal with Whop.
