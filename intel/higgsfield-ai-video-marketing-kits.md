# Higgsfield AI, and how an AI video platform's output and its promoters can be told apart from the real thing

Date: 2026-10-24. Status: researched, public sources plus one re-measurement of the vendor's public record against my 24 Sep note.

## What it is

Higgsfield is a hosted AI video and image service that routes prompts to other companies' models (Veo, Kling, Seedance and others) behind its own presets: camera moves, "Soul" stills, lip-sync, an AI Influencer Studio that puts a made-up character into existing footage, and a Marketing Studio that turns a product URL into ad clips. It is a real company, Higgsfield Inc. of San Francisco with "a location in Kazakhstan" (its own privacy policy), and the money is real: a $400m Series B at $5.4bn in August 2026, up from $1.3bn in January, with a stated annualised run rate of $700m measured on the latest four weeks. It reached me on 24 Sep through an Instagram reel that turned out to be an affiliate post. What makes it an intelligence subject is not the model quality; it is that the company grew by paying tens of thousands of creators to post its clips, that Forbes found the kits it handed them contained stock footage relabelled as AI and deepfakes of real people, and that the same tool is now used to front memecoins with characters nobody can be held to.

## What it costs

Subscriptions sold as credit allowances. Higgsfield's own pages gave $15, $39 and $99 a month on annual billing for 200, 1,000 and 3,000 credits in April 2026; a third-party guide this month quotes $19, $47 and $99 annual for 270, 1,200 and 3,000, so the lower tiers move. Subscription credits expire at the end of each billing period; add-on credits expire after 90 days; refunds only within seven days and only if no credit was used, less a fee of up to 6%. Free accounts get a visible watermark on every file; any paid plan removes it. Affiliates earn up to 25% of what a referral pays for up to 12 months, and the referral link gives the newcomer up to 50% off for three hours after login, which is why the reels say "link in bio, today". I paid nothing and made no account.

## How it works

The parts a defender needs, not the prompt guide.

- **Model routing plus presets.** Higgsfield trains some of its own components but the heavy lifting is third-party models under licence, wrapped in named looks. That matters for provenance: a watermark baked in by Google's Veo (SynthID) would travel with a Veo-routed clip whatever Higgsfield does on top, while a Kling- or Seedance-routed clip carries whatever those vendors add. Higgsfield does not say which model made which file.
- **Paid distribution at scale.** Forbes (12 Feb 2026, Rashi Shrivastava) reported that the Higgsfield Earn programme commissioned 10,000 creators who submitted 50,000 videos in its first 20 days, paid by shares and likes, and that the marketing team offered a London game director $200 to post a kit clip. The kit clips he was sent included Envato stock templates with the logo added, not AI output. Other creators were sent Drive folders with racist clips of children's characters and nonconsensual deepfakes of Sydney Sweeney, Zendaya and Donald Trump. The chief strategy officer, Mahi de Silva, called the kit a mistake made "for ideation purposes" and said the company's X account had been suspended for "inauthentic behavior". The company also said it shut about 40,000 accounts and refunded $1.35m after throttling discounted "unlimited" plans it blamed partly on bots.
- **Training on everything by default.** The terms (updated 23 Oct 2026, the day before this note) say Your Content, Inputs and Outputs "may be used by Company to train, develop, enhance, evolve, and improve" its models; the only opt-out is deleting the content or the account, and "Content already incorporated into our AI models before deletion cannot feasibly be removed". Enterprise agreements are excluded. The face and voice of anyone a user uploads goes in unless the user is on Enterprise.
- **Provenance promised, not guaranteed.** The same terms say the company "may embed machine-readable watermarks, secure metadata, or content-provenance signals (such as those based on the C2PA / Content Credentials standard)", may make them imperceptible, and "does not warrant that any marking will be applied to, or persist in, every Output". Users must not remove them. The help centre repeats that invisible markers "aren't removed by upgrading". So a paid user gets a clean-looking file that may or may not carry a signed manifest.
- **Characters as fronts.** Semafor reported in October (secondary coverage only; I could not open the piece) that users are putting Higgsfield's AI characters in front of memecoin promotions. A character has no name to sue, no face to recognise and no account history, which is exactly the property a pump wants in a spokesperson.

## How it is detected

In the order a platform, a buyer or a reader meets them.

1. **Check the file for a manifest before trusting the look.** A C2PA viewer (c2pa.org's verify page, or the open `c2patool`) reads the signed manifest if one survived. Three outcomes: a Higgsfield or Google manifest present (AI, and from which route), a manifest that fails validation (edited after signing, which itself is a signal), or none. None proves nothing: Higgsfield says marking is not guaranteed, and every re-encode by Instagram, TikTok or CapCut strips metadata. Google's SynthID detector only answers for Google-made content, so a Veo-routed clip may be detectable there when a Kling-routed one is not. The honest state of play is that a clean Higgsfield export on a paid plan is, to a viewer, indistinguishable from footage unless a marker survived, and the company itself will not warrant that it did.
2. **The promoter's shape, not the clip.** The account that sent me the 24 Sep reel had no product of its own, a 50%-off-today hook, and the partner hashtag. Affiliates earning 25% for a year are advertisers; in the UK a trader posing as an ordinary user is banned outright under DMCC 2024 Schedule 20 (practice 25) with no harm test, and the ASA's rule is that a paid post says so up front. Forbes' detail is the usable one: kit footage lacking the artefacts the tool actually produces was what tipped the creator off, so a batch of "AI" clips that look too clean, too uniform or too much like stock is a reason to look for the Envato original, which reverse image search finds.
3. **Review drift, measured.** On 24 Sep I noted Trustpilot at 4.0 over about 4,200 reviews with 19% one-star. On 24 Oct it reads 4.0 over 5,152 with 18% one-star (63% five-star), profile claimed, paid Trustpilot subscription, 96% of negative reviews answered within a week with replies Trustpilot flags as possibly AI-drafted. So roughly 950 reviews in a month, the one-star share flat, and the five most recent one-stars (15 to 24 Oct) are all the same three themes: surprise on-demand credit charges and a second charge inside a month, Unlimited plans that queue and fail, and support that does not resolve. Steady, not falling: a company that answers everything and keeps growing, with the same complaint rate it had a month ago. The complaint shape (credits that expire, auto-refill below 500 credits on the higher plans) is the business model, not a bug.
4. **Platform-side behaviour signals.** Higgsfield's own X account was suspended for inauthentic behaviour, by de Silva's account, which is the platform catching coordinated posting from the kit rather than catching the clips. That is the general lesson from this and from Eromify last week: platforms are moving from image forensics to behaviour (posting cadence, identical captions across accounts, partner tags before an audience exists), and Instagram is reported to be moving to compulsory labels for AI-generated influencer profiles with reduced reach for the unlabelled.
5. **Memecoin fronts.** A token promoted by a face that has no history outside this month's posts, speaks in Soul-style stills and short lip-synced clips, and never appears live is a character, and the Grinder's own gate already treats "no doxxed team" as a risk flag. The detection is a question, not a tool: can this spokesperson be found anywhere before the token existed?

What does not work: judging the clip by eye (the better models passed that bar in 2026), star averages from search snippets, and the vendor's own partner testimonials. What works: a manifest check, the promoter's incentive, the dated one-star themes, and asking whether the face existed last month.

## What I did not run, and why

No account, no generation, no download of a sample clip, so I did not run a C2PA check on a real Higgsfield export. That is the one measurement that would settle whether the "may embed" is "does embed", and it needs an account, which is off limits for me. A reader with a paid plan can do it in a minute with `c2patool` on one fresh export, and the result belongs in this file. I did not confirm the Semafor memecoin report at source, and the Wikipedia Content Credentials table listing Higgsfield as "implemented 2026-07, no conformance" is unverified and I have not leaned on it.
