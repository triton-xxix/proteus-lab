# Zupria, and courses that teach an "AI OnlyFans model", read as a defender

Date: 2026-11-18. Status: researched, public sources only, nothing bought, generated or posted. Seed: the Zupria course (SEEN.md, 27 Sep 2026). Owed from Sat 14 Nov, when no nightly ran (F-0100).

## What it is
A paid video course, sold by Facebook ad, that teaches a buyer to build an AI-generated woman and
sell subscriptions to her. The Zupria one has 19 modules and a traceable seller (Bogdan Irimia,
Zupria SRL, Braila, Romania, VAT RO40257322, as read on 27 Sep 2026). It sits upstream of the
things already written up here: the generators (`intel/eromify-ai-companion-funnels.md`), the
personas sold ready-made (`intel/synthetic-influencer-personas.md`) and the phones that post them
(`intel/butter-antidetect-cloud-phones.md`). This one sells the idea and the method, not the tool.

## What it costs
77 euros "lifetime", struck through from 397. The course then requires about 79 euros a month of
generation-app subscriptions, with 42 more optional, so a year is about 950 euros before any
advertising. I paid nothing; no SPEND.md line.

## How it works
At the level a defender needs. The buyer fixes one face, generates stills and clips of it, opens a
subscription page, and drives traffic from short-video platforms. The course's commercial trick is
in its name: it is titled for OnlyFans, the platform people have heard of, while its 30-day
earnings guarantee is conditioned on Fanvue, the platform that openly takes AI creators. The two
have opposite rules, and the course's product only fits one of them. It also carries a module on
deepfake techniques, with no legal warning on the page.

## How it is detected
- **The platform mismatch.** OnlyFans is reported (Reuters, its chief executive, several secondary
  guides; I did not open the terms themselves) to allow AI content only of the verified account
  holder, disclosed. A wholly synthetic woman with a different verified owner behind her is the
  thing that rule exists to stop. So an "AI OnlyFans model" is, on OnlyFans, a terms breach waiting
  for the identity check: the face on the page will not match the ID on file.
- **The label.** Fanvue's own help centre says AI content is allowed under section 10.3 of its
  terms, must be disclosed, that AI creator accounts are verified like human ones and carry an AI
  tag, and gives a report route for an unlabelled one. An untagged account posting a face that
  never ages is the reportable case. Secondary sources add a cap of 15 AI accounts per owner
  against 2 human; unverified.
- **The ad.** The funnel starts on Meta. A page that promises income, names a platform it cannot
  lawfully deliver on, and strikes through a price is three things UK ad rules already cover:
  income claims need evidence, and a "was" price must have been charged. The DMCC bounds are in the
  Eromify write-up.
- **The law, which is the strong lever in the UK.** Creating a sexually explicit image of a real
  adult without consent has been an offence at the point of creation since 6 February 2026
  (Sexual Offences Act 2003 sections 66E to 66H, inserted by section 138 of the Data (Use and
  Access) Act 2025, commenced by SI 2026/31). Requesting one is 66F. A course module teaching face
  swaps onto real people, with no warning, walks a UK buyer toward that offence. Age assurance for
  pornographic content has been required since July 2025 and the course gives no guidance on it.
- **The seller's own trail.** A VAT number, a registered company and a named founder are unusual
  in this category and cut both ways: the seller is reachable by a regulator, and the claims on
  the page are attributable.

What does not work: judging the output by image faults, and assuming the name on the tin says where
the accounts end up. Follow the guarantee's small print, not the title.
